Purpose & Scope
This handbook supports standardized preoperative medical evaluation for adult patients undergoing elective noncardiac surgery at a spine and joint specialty hospital. It is designed to reduce avoidable day-of-surgery cancellations, identify conditions requiring additional evaluation, and create a consistent record of medical optimization.
Optimized
Medical conditions are sufficiently evaluated and managed to proceed under current institutional criteria.
Pending
Additional records, testing, treatment, or specialty input is required before final optimization status.
Not optimized
A clinically significant condition or unresolved risk exceeds facility or perioperative parameters.
Preoperative Evaluation Workflow
1
Define procedure
Confirm procedure, surgeon, surgery date, laterality, anticipated surgical risk, and expected disposition.
2
Review history
Identify active symptoms, cardiopulmonary disease, prior anesthesia problems, thromboembolic history, medication risks, and recent hospitalization.
3
Apply screening tools
Document DASI, RCRI, STOP-BANG, Clinical Frailty Scale, and pulmonary tools when applicable.
4
Review diagnostics
Interpret laboratory results, EKG, imaging, and prior specialty testing rather than simply listing results.
5
Resolve abnormalities
Address clinically relevant abnormalities in the Assessment & Plan and identify whether they require treatment, surveillance, records, or referral.
6
Assign final status
Use Optimized, Pending, or Not optimized, with the reason when not fully optimized.
Preoperative Cardiac Evaluation
Core principle: Additional testing should be driven by active cardiac conditions, estimated perioperative risk, functional capacity, and whether the result would change management.
Active cardiac problems requiring attention
Evaluate for acute coronary syndrome or unstable ischemic symptoms, decompensated heart failure, clinically significant uncontrolled arrhythmias, symptomatic severe valvular disease, and other unstable cardiovascular conditions before elective surgery.
Cardiac testing already on file
Summarize the study date, test type, major findings, interpretation, and perioperative relevance. Avoid ordering repeat testing solely because a study is old when there has been no meaningful clinical change and repeat testing would not alter management.
RCRI & BNP Screening Protocol
TSJH / TSJR BNP screening trigger
Obtain BNP or NT-proBNP when the patient meets the institutional screening criteria, including age and cardiac-risk thresholds defined by current protocol.
BNP≥ 92 pg/mL
NT-proBNP≥ 300 ng/L
If the applicable threshold is met, use the postoperative cardiac-surveillance pathway: postoperative EKG and daily troponin surveillance for 48–72 hours, consistent with current institutional protocol.
RCRI elements
The Revised Cardiac Risk Index is based on six traditional predictors: high-risk surgery, ischemic heart disease, history of heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, insulin-treated diabetes, and preoperative creatinine greater than 2 mg/dL. Record the numeric score in the preoperative note.
EKG Interpretation & Escalation
Usually requires focused review
New ischemic changes, pathologic rhythm disturbance, significant conduction disease, unexplained tachycardia or bradycardia, prolonged QT in the appropriate clinical context, or findings that represent a meaningful change from baseline.
LBBB
For left bundle branch block requiring ischemic evaluation, exercise ECG alone is generally not an appropriate ischemia test. Follow institutional guidance for pharmacologic nuclear stress testing or cardiology evaluation when further evaluation is indicated.

Heart Failure / Cardiomyopathy
Determine current symptoms, recent decompensation or admission, ejection fraction when known, NYHA functional class, guideline-directed medical therapy, volume status, and whether the patient meets facility-specific parameters.
Higher-level-of-care consideration: Markedly reduced EF, recent decompensated heart failure, active symptoms, or other evidence of poor cardiac reserve may require a higher-acuity surgical setting.
Pulmonary Disease & Obstructive Sleep Apnea
OSA
Record STOP-BANG and PAP use. Patients prescribed PAP should bring and use PAP whenever asleep unless contraindicated. When PAP is not tolerated, define a postoperative monitoring plan such as continuous pulse oximetry and respiratory support if clinically warranted.
COPD / chronic lung disease
Assess baseline oxygen requirement, recent exacerbation, symptoms, functional limitation, inhaler adherence, and available pulmonary testing. Use CAT, BODE, Arozullah, PFT information, and institutional exclusion criteria when applicable.
Pulmonary hypertension
Identify pulmonary hypertension group when known, RV function, tricuspid regurgitation severity, estimated pulmonary pressures, symptoms, and pulmonary vasodilator therapy. Significant RV dysfunction or severe pulmonary hypertension warrants escalation.

Abnormal UA & Urine Culture
A positive urinalysis alone does not establish a symptomatic urinary tract infection. Interpret urinary symptoms, systemic findings, culture result, organism burden, procedure type, and institutional policy together.
Assess for true infection
Dysuria, urgency/frequency beyond baseline, suprapubic symptoms, flank pain, fever, systemic illness, or other compatible findings increase concern.
Avoid reflex treatment
Asymptomatic bacteriuria should not automatically trigger antibiotics or cancellation for every orthopedic or spine patient. Follow the current surgical and infection-prevention protocol.
Abnormal Laboratory Results
Finding
Preoperative approach
Anemia
Characterize severity and likely cause; consider iron studies or additional evaluation when clinically indicated and document blood-loss implications.
Thrombocytopenia / thrombocytosis
Review trend, severity, cause, bleeding/thrombotic history, and procedural implications.
Renal dysfunction
Compare baseline, assess volume status and medication implications, adjust anticoagulant considerations, and identify possible acute kidney injury.
Electrolyte abnormality
Determine severity, symptoms, arrhythmia risk, likely cause, and whether correction is required before surgery.
Hyperglycemia / elevated A1c
Assess current control, medication plan, infection risk, and institutional thresholds.
Functional Capacity, Frailty & Screening Scores
DASI
Use the self-reported Duke Activity Status Index for functional-capacity assessment and record the numeric result.
RCRI
Record numeric cardiac-risk points and apply the institutional BNP pathway when indicated.
STOP-BANG
Record the total score and pair it with actual OSA diagnosis, PAP use, and postoperative monitoring needs.
CFS
Record the Clinical Frailty Scale score and use it to support disposition and postoperative risk planning.

Postoperative Medical Planning
Preoperative optimization should include anticipated postoperative needs. Address respiratory monitoring, PAP use, cardiac surveillance when triggered, diabetes management, medication resumption, anticoagulation/antiplatelet strategy, bowel regimen, nausea risk, delirium/frailty risk, and likely disposition.
Documentation Standards
Required optimization statement
Use a final status of Optimized, Pending, or Not optimized, with the reason when applicable.

Patient meets TSJH perioperative consensus guidelines to proceed with elective surgery on scheduled surgery date at this time.
