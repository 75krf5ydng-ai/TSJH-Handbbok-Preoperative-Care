Same-Day Cancellation Prevention & Final Readiness Workflow

Pending-item ownership, communication standards and the final 24–48 hour surgical readiness check

Home Evaluation Communication Questionnaires Testing Windows Resources

Purpose

Many same-day surgical cancellations are preventable when abnormal findings, missing records, medication holds and specialist recommendations are identified early and assigned to a specific team member.

The goal is not simply to complete a preoperative note. The goal is to ensure that every clinically meaningful issue has been identified, addressed, communicated and resolved before the patient arrives for surgery.

The Core Rule: Every Pending Item Needs an Owner

A pending item without an assigned owner is at high risk of becoming a same-day cancellation.
For every pending item document:

What is pending?
Who owns it?
When is it due?
Who must review the result?
Who communicates the final decision?
What happens if the item is not completed?

Common Causes of Preventable Same-Day Cancellation

  • Anticoagulant or antiplatelet not held appropriately
  • SGLT2 inhibitor not held according to protocol
  • Unresolved GLP-1 aspiration-risk concern
  • Missing cardiac testing or cardiology records
  • Unresolved abnormal EKG
  • Missing pacemaker/ICD interrogation
  • Unresolved severe hypertension
  • Unreviewed abnormal labs
  • Active infection or wound discovered late
  • Recent stroke/TIA timing not recognized
  • Unclear dialysis timing
  • Missing specialty recommendation
  • Unsafe postoperative disposition
  • Patient did not understand fasting or medication instructions

Communication Standard

Communication should identify the problem, what has been done, what remains pending and what decision is required.

Patient: ___
Procedure/date: ___
Issue: ___

Avoid Vague Requests

Avoid:

“Needs cardiac clearance.”
“Abnormal labs—please advise.”
“Needs anesthesia review.”
“Patient has heart history.”
Prefer:

“Patient scheduled for multilevel lumbar fusion on ___. History of HFrEF with EF 40%. Last cardiology visit was ___ and patient is clinically stable without recent CHF admission. Please advise whether current cardiac status/testing is adequate for planned elective noncardiac surgery and provide perioperative cardiac recommendations.”

Level Example Action

Day-Before Patient Contact

When part of the facility workflow, a brief day-before contact can identify changes that might otherwise become a day-of-surgery problem.

Ask

  • Any fever?
  • Any cough or respiratory illness?
  • Any chest pain?
  • Any new shortness of breath?
  • Any ER visit or hospitalization?
  • Any new medication?
  • Any skin infection or wound?
  • Did you stop medications exactly as instructed?
  • Do you understand fasting instructions?
  • Do you have your PAP device if instructed?
  • Do you have your walker/assistive device if required?
  • Is transportation/home support confirmed?

Optimization Status Definitions

Status Meaning
Optimized Medical evaluation is complete and no unresolved issue currently prevents proceeding under the planned perioperative pathway.
Pending One or more required items remain unresolved. The patient may ultimately proceed, but final determination cannot yet be made.
Not optimized A current medical issue requires treatment, delay, higher-level planning or other intervention before elective surgery.
Do not label a patient “Optimized” while a clinically meaningful required test, specialist recommendation or medication plan remains unresolved.

Same-Day Discovery Workflow

If an unexpected issue is discovered on the day of surgery, communicate quickly and specifically rather than allowing multiple teams to work from incomplete information.

Unexpected issue identified
↓
Assess clinical urgency
↓
Notify appropriate provider/anesthesia/surgeon
↓
Determine whether issue can be safely resolved today
↓
If yes → document management and proceed if appropriate
↓
If no → postpone/cancel with clear medical rationale
↓
Assign follow-up plan before patient leaves

Team Huddle for Complex Patients

A brief multidisciplinary discussion may prevent late cancellation for patients with multiple interacting risks.

Consider a Preoperative Huddle For

  • Complex cardiac disease
  • Severe pulmonary disease
  • Pulmonary hypertension
  • Recent stroke/TIA
  • Complex anticoagulation
  • Pacemaker/ICD with significant EMI concerns
  • Dialysis
  • Major blood-refusal surgery
  • Severe frailty
  • High-risk complex spine surgery

Complex-Patient Huddle Questions

What is the highest-risk issue?

Is the patient medically optimized?

Is TSJH the appropriate surgical venue?

Does anesthesia agree with the plan?

Is cardiac/pulmonary/device monitoring sufficient?

Is anticoagulation management finalized?

Is blood-management planning adequate?

What postoperative level of care is expected?

What would cause cancellation on DOS?

Can that issue be resolved before DOS?

Chart Organization

Important perioperative information should be easy to find.

Place the most relevant specialist recommendation, cardiac testing summary, antithrombotic plan, device plan and final optimization status where the surgical and anesthesia teams can readily locate them.

Avoid Duplicate Work

Before ordering a new test or requesting another specialty visit, determine whether adequate recent information already exists and whether repeating it is likely to change perioperative management.

Efficient records review reduces unnecessary testing while still allowing clinically important gaps to be identified.

Track Results to Closure

Ordering a repeat test is not the same as resolving the issue. Someone must review the result and update the patient's final status.
Test ordered
↓
Test completed
↓
Result reviewed
↓
Action taken if abnormal
↓
Optimization status updated
↓
Team notified when needed

Documentation Example — Pending

FINAL PREOPERATIVE OPTIMIZATION STATUS: Pending. Patient requires updated pacemaker interrogation and anesthesia review because pacemaker dependence is uncertain and monopolar electrocautery is anticipated during the planned procedure. Device interrogation has been requested and assigned to ___. Final status will be updated after the device/anesthesia plan is reviewed.

Documentation Example — Resolved

Previously pending cardiac/device information has been received and reviewed. Interrogation demonstrates acceptable battery and lead function and the perioperative device plan has been confirmed with anesthesia. No remaining medical optimization issue identified. FINAL PREOPERATIVE OPTIMIZATION STATUS: Optimized.

Key Takeaways

  • Every pending item must have a named owner.
  • Ordering a test does not resolve the issue until the result is reviewed.
  • Use specific clinical questions instead of generic “clearance” requests.
  • Verify high-risk medication holds before the day of surgery.
  • Review device, dialysis and anticoagulation plans early.
  • Use a 72-hour readiness review for unresolved items.
  • Use a final 24–48 hour check for interval health changes and completed holds.
  • Do not label the patient Optimized while required items remain pending.
  • Pending and Not optimized statuses must include a reason.
  • Unsafe disposition can be a real perioperative barrier.
  • Complex patients may benefit from a brief multidisciplinary preoperative huddle.
  • Same-day cancellations should generate a documented follow-up plan.
  • Track cancellation reasons so recurrent workflow problems can be corrected.
