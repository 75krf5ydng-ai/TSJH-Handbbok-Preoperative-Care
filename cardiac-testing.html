Cardiac Testing Before Elective Noncardiac Surgery

Preoperative testing, interpretation, timing, and perioperative decision support

Home Preop Evaluation Cardiac EKG

Purpose

Preoperative cardiac testing should be ordered only when the result is expected to change perioperative management, surgical timing, medical therapy, level of care, or the need for additional cardiovascular evaluation.

Key Principle: Do not order routine cardiac testing solely because surgery is planned. Use the same indications for cardiovascular testing that would apply outside the perioperative setting.

Stepwise Cardiac Testing Approach

  1. Determine urgency of surgery.
  2. Identify active or unstable cardiac conditions.
  3. Determine surgical procedural risk.
  4. Calculate RCRI.
  5. Assess functional capacity using self-reported DASI.
  6. Determine whether BNP or NT-proBNP screening is indicated.
  7. Review prior cardiac testing and determine whether repeat testing is necessary.
  8. Order additional testing only if results are expected to alter management.

Active Cardiac Conditions

Elective surgery should generally be deferred for evaluation and treatment when an unstable cardiovascular condition is present.

Examples requiring further evaluation:
  • Acute coronary syndrome or suspected active myocardial ischemia
  • Decompensated heart failure
  • Unstable or clinically significant arrhythmia
  • Symptomatic severe valvular heart disease
  • New unexplained cardiopulmonary symptoms concerning for cardiac disease

12-Lead Electrocardiogram

Consider Preoperative EKG

  • Known coronary artery disease
  • Significant arrhythmia
  • Peripheral arterial disease
  • Cerebrovascular disease
  • Structural heart disease
  • Significant cardiac symptoms
  • Elevated-risk surgery when clinically appropriate

Routine EKG Usually Not Needed

  • Asymptomatic low-risk patient
  • Low-risk surgery without cardiac disease
  • No clinical indication outside the surgical setting
New EKG abnormality: Compare with prior tracing when available. New ischemic changes, significant conduction abnormalities, concerning arrhythmias, or unexplained abnormalities may require additional evaluation before elective surgery.
Left Bundle Branch Block: For elective surgery, a newly identified or clinically significant LBBB should prompt cardiovascular evaluation or appropriate ischemic assessment. If nuclear stress testing is selected, pharmacologic vasodilator testing such as regadenoson is generally preferred over an exercise ECG-based ischemia assessment because LBBB may interfere with interpretation.

Echocardiography

Routine repeat echocardiography is not required in every patient with known cardiac disease. Order or obtain an updated study when the information may change perioperative management.

Clinical Situation Preoperative Consideration
New or worsening dyspnea Consider assessment of ventricular and valvular function.
Suspected new heart failure Evaluate ventricular function before elective surgery when clinically indicated.
Known heart failure with clinical deterioration Updated echocardiography may be appropriate.
Clinically stable heart failure with recent adequate study Routine repeat testing is generally unnecessary solely because surgery is planned.
Suspected moderate or severe valvular disease Obtain or review recent echocardiographic assessment and cardiovascular evaluation as appropriate.
Stable known valve disease with adequate recent assessment Repeat testing depends on interval, symptoms, severity, and expected progression.

Aortic Stenosis

Patients with moderate-to-severe or symptomatic aortic stenosis require careful perioperative assessment. Confirm severity, symptoms, ventricular function, and cardiovascular recommendations before elective surgery.

Severe Aortic Stenosis Parameters:
  • Aortic valve area < 1.0 cm²
  • Mean gradient > 40 mmHg
  • Peak velocity > 4.0 m/s

Severe or symptomatic aortic stenosis may exceed the capabilities of a freestanding orthopedic or spine hospital and may require higher-level cardiovascular and anesthetic resources.

Mitral Stenosis

Moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis should have appropriate cardiovascular evaluation and recent assessment of severity, symptoms, pulmonary pressures, rhythm, and ventricular function.

Findings consistent with severe disease may include:
  • Mitral valve area ≤ 1.5 cm²
  • Mean mitral gradient ≥ 10 mmHg
  • Pulmonary artery systolic pressure > 50 mmHg

Heart Failure and Cardiomyopathy

Cardiovascular risk increases in patients with heart failure, particularly with reduced ejection fraction, recent decompensation, or active symptoms.

Evaluate:
  • Recent heart-failure symptoms or admissions
  • Ejection fraction
  • NYHA functional class
  • Guideline-directed medical therapy
  • Volume status
  • Functional capacity
  • Recent cardiology recommendations
Higher-Level-of-Care Consideration: Severely reduced LV systolic function, especially an EF below approximately 35%, requires individualized cardiovascular and anesthesia review and may not be appropriate for a limited-resource surgical facility.

Stress Testing

Stress testing should not be performed routinely before surgery.

Consider stress testing only when:
  • The patient has elevated perioperative cardiovascular risk, AND
  • Functional capacity is poor or unknown, AND
  • The result would change management.

A patient with acceptable functional capacity, stable symptoms, and no other indication for ischemic evaluation generally does not need stress testing solely for preoperative risk stratification.

After a Normal Stress Test

A normal stress test does not itself create a mandatory waiting period before noncardiac surgery. Once the study has been reviewed, the patient is clinically stable, and no additional intervention or evaluation is required, surgery may proceed according to the overall perioperative plan.

Coronary CT Angiography

Coronary CT angiography is not a routine screening study for preoperative patients. It may be considered in selected patients when coronary anatomy needs clarification and the result is expected to change management.

Consider renal function, contrast exposure, coronary calcium burden, heart rate, and whether another testing strategy would be more appropriate.

Cardiac Catheterization / Coronary Angiography

Invasive coronary angiography should not be performed solely because noncardiac surgery is planned. The indication should be the same as it would be in a patient who was not preparing for surgery.

If catheterization shows no obstructive disease requiring intervention: there is no automatic waiting period solely because diagnostic angiography was performed. Proceed when clinically stable, the access site is satisfactory, renal function is acceptable when relevant, and the cardiology plan has been reviewed.
If PCI or another cardiac intervention is performed, surgical timing changes and must account for stent type, indication for PCI, antiplatelet therapy, bleeding risk, and the urgency of noncardiac surgery.

Prior PCI and Coronary Stents

Determine the date, indication, and type of coronary intervention before recommending interruption of antiplatelet therapy.

Information Needed Why It Matters
Date of PCI Determines whether elective surgery should be delayed.
Reason for PCI Acute coronary syndrome carries different risk than stable coronary disease.
Stent type Helps determine antiplatelet requirements and recommended timing.
Aspirin / P2Y12 therapy Stopping therapy too early may cause stent thrombosis.
Planned surgical bleeding risk Must be balanced against thrombotic risk.
Do not independently discontinue dual antiplatelet therapy in a recently stented patient. Coordinate with cardiology, anesthesia, and the surgeon.

BNP / NT-proBNP Screening

Natriuretic peptide testing can help identify patients at increased risk for postoperative cardiovascular events when clinical risk is elevated.

TSJR / TSJH Screening Trigger:
Obtain BNP or NT-proBNP when:
  • RCRI ≥ 1, OR
  • Age ≥ 65 years, OR
  • Age 45–64 years with significant cardiovascular disease.
Biomarker Threshold Postoperative Plan
BNP ≥ 92 pg/mL Postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.
NT-proBNP ≥ 300 ng/L Postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.
Below threshold BNP < 92 or NT-proBNP < 300 No routine postoperative cardiac-surveillance protocol based solely on biomarker result.
SmartPhrase:

BNP screening. Obtain BNP because of age greater than 65 years and RCRI score of 1. If BNP is 92 pg/mL or greater, implement the TSJH postoperative cardiac-surveillance protocol with postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.

Postoperative Troponin Surveillance

Patients meeting institutional high-risk criteria may require postoperative surveillance for myocardial injury even when they do not report classic ischemic symptoms.

When indicated by institutional protocol:
  • Obtain postoperative EKG.
  • Obtain daily troponin levels for 48–72 hours.
  • Evaluate abnormal values in the clinical context.
  • Assess for ischemia, arrhythmia, anemia, hypotension, hypoxia, sepsis, or other causes of myocardial injury.

When Prior Cardiac Testing May Be Sufficient

Do not automatically repeat a test merely because it was performed months earlier. Review whether the patient's clinical condition has changed.

Usually No Repeat Needed

  • No new symptoms
  • No significant change in functional capacity
  • Known stable disease
  • Prior study adequately answers the clinical question
  • No expected change in management

Consider Updated Testing

  • New chest pain
  • New or worsening dyspnea
  • Syncope or concerning presyncope
  • Heart-failure exacerbation
  • New murmur or suspected valve progression
  • New significant EKG abnormality
  • Major decline in functional status

Cardiology Consultation

Cardiology consultation is most useful when a specific question needs to be answered, rather than requesting nonspecific “cardiac clearance.”

Examples of Appropriate Questions

  • Is additional ischemic testing indicated?
  • Is the patient's heart failure medically optimized?
  • Does this valve lesion require intervention before surgery?
  • Can antiplatelet therapy be interrupted safely?
  • Does this arrhythmia require additional treatment before surgery?
  • Is the patient suitable for this surgical facility or should surgery occur at a higher level of care?
Preferred documentation: State whether the patient is medically optimized, whether additional cardiac testing is required, and any specific perioperative medication, monitoring, or level-of-care recommendations.

Testing That Should Not Be Ordered Routinely

  • Echocardiogram in every preoperative patient
  • Stress test solely because of age
  • Stress test solely because orthopedic or spine surgery is planned
  • Coronary angiography solely to obtain surgical clearance
  • Repeat testing when a recent adequate study exists and there has been no clinical change
  • Routine cardiac biomarkers in low-risk patients without an indication

Preoperative Provider Quick Decision Guide

Finding Action
No cardiac symptoms, low procedural risk Proceed without additional routine cardiac testing when otherwise appropriate.
Good functional capacity and stable cardiac disease Additional ischemic testing generally not required solely for surgery.
Poor or unknown functional capacity with elevated cardiac risk Consider BNP/NT-proBNP and selective further testing if results will change management.
New chest pain or suspected ACS Defer elective surgery and obtain urgent cardiovascular evaluation.
Decompensated heart failure Defer elective surgery until evaluated and optimized.
New significant arrhythmia Evaluate before elective surgery.
Moderate-to-severe valve disease Confirm recent echo and cardiovascular assessment.
Normal stress test with no intervention required No test-related mandatory waiting interval; proceed when overall clinical evaluation is complete.
Diagnostic cardiac catheterization without intervention No automatic surgical waiting period; verify stability, access-site status, renal function when applicable, and cardiology recommendations.
PCI/stent performed Do not proceed based on diagnostic cath timing rules. Follow PCI-specific surgical timing and antiplatelet guidance.

Documentation Checklist

For patients with cardiovascular disease, document:

  • Cardiac diagnoses
  • Current symptoms
  • RCRI score
  • DASI score
  • Active cardiac problems: Yes / No
  • Surgical risk: Low / Intermediate / High
  • BNP screening: Yes / No
  • Date and interpretation of most recent EKG
  • Date and interpretation of echocardiogram when applicable
  • Date and interpretation of stress testing when applicable
  • Date and findings of cardiac catheterization or PCI when applicable
  • Most recent cardiology visit and recommendations
  • Perioperative medication recommendations
  • Need for postoperative cardiac surveillance
  • Final preoperative optimization status

Final Preoperative Status

Optimized

Cardiac evaluation is complete, no additional testing or treatment is required, and the patient meets applicable perioperative criteria to proceed.

Pending

Additional cardiac records, testing, specialist recommendations, or medication instructions are required before final determination.

Not Optimized

An active or inadequately evaluated cardiovascular condition requires treatment, additional evaluation, postponement, or consideration of a higher level of care.

Clinical Reminder

This handbook is intended to support clinical decision-making and does not replace individualized physician, anesthesia, cardiology, or surgeon judgment. Apply current institutional policy and national perioperative cardiovascular guidelines when making patient-specific decisions.
