Purpose

This page serves as the central access point for the handbook's interactive clinical tools and commonly used external decision-support resources.

Use calculators to support clinical reasoning, not replace it. Always interpret results with symptoms, surgical risk, expected blood loss, current medications, renal function and applicable facility pathways.

Diabetes Tools

The most important perioperative diabetes “calculator” is often a medication and metabolic-risk review rather than a single numeric score.

Always assess

  • Diabetes type
  • A1c
  • Current glucose
  • SGLT2 use and last dose
  • GLP-1/GIP use and GI symptoms
  • Basal insulin
  • Prandial insulin
  • Pump/CGM
  • DKA/HHS history

When NOT to Use a Calculator Alone

Do not rely on a score alone when there is:

Active chest pain
Decompensated heart failure
Unstable arrhythmia
Acute stroke symptoms
DKA/HHS
Active systemic infection
Severe pulmonary decompensation
Active bleeding
Other acute medical instability

These conditions require direct clinical evaluation rather than risk-score reassurance.

Important Disclaimer

External calculators, online evidence tools and handbook calculators are decision-support aids. They do not supersede patient-specific clinical judgment, current facility policies, anesthesia requirements, surgeon-specific bleeding protocols or specialist recommendations when those are applicable.

Key Takeaways

  • Use one central page to access commonly used calculators and evidence tools.
  • MDCalc supports—not replaces—clinical judgment.
  • OpenEvidence is useful for focused questions not fully addressed in the handbook.
  • DASI is preferred for self-reported functional-capacity assessment.
  • RCRI should be interpreted with active cardiac conditions and procedure risk.
  • BNP/NT-proBNP screening may trigger postoperative cardiac surveillance.
  • Use the anemia calculator for CBC/iron-pattern differentiation.
  • Use the PFT guide for obstruction/restriction/diffusion interpretation.
  • Use the EKG guide to distinguish stable chronic findings from changes requiring workup.
  • For anticoagulation, account for renal function and procedure bleeding consequence.
  • No calculator should override an active unstable medical condition.
