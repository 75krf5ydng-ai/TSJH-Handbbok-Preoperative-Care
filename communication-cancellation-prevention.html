Communication, Collaboration & Same-Day Cancellation Prevention

Standardizing handoffs, ownership, escalation and readiness across the preoperative team

Home RN Workflow Provider Workflow Anesthesia Final Readiness Resources

Purpose

Same-day cancellations are often preventable when clinically important issues are identified early, clearly communicated and assigned to a responsible person before the patient arrives for surgery.

The most effective preoperative workflow is not simply “more testing.” It is clear ownership, timely review, concise communication and closure of unresolved items.

Core Communication Principle

Every unresolved item should have four things: a clearly stated problem, a specific requested action, an assigned owner and a defined follow-up point.
“Cardiology pending,” “needs anesthesia review,” or “abnormal labs” are not complete plans.

Preoperative Team Roles

Team Member Primary Role
RN Collect, verify, screen, summarize, initiate approved protocols and escalate abnormalities.
Preoperative provider Interpret findings, risk stratify, optimize medical conditions and determine need for escalation.
Anesthesia Address airway, anesthetic, aspiration, neuraxial, device, respiratory and venue-specific questions.
Surgeon Procedure-specific timing, bleeding consequences, DVT prophylaxis, postoperative restrictions and operative decisions.
Specialist Address unresolved disease-specific questions outside routine preoperative scope.
Scheduler / office staff Coordinate dates, records, patient instructions and communication between teams.

Preventing Communication Gaps

Every clinically significant issue should answer:

What is the problem?
Why does it matter for surgery?
What information is still missing?
Who is responsible for obtaining it?
Who will review it?
By when does it need to be completed?
What happens if it is not completed?

Good vs Poor Escalation

Poor:

“Abnormal EKG. Please review.”
Better:

“Patient scheduled for elective TKA on ___. EKG today shows new LBBB compared with tracing from ___. Patient denies chest pain or dyspnea. DASI ___. No recent ischemic testing is available. Please review for TSJH LBBB pathway and determine whether additional cardiovascular evaluation is required before surgery.”

Communication With Cardiology

Avoid generic “cardiac clearance” requests.
Ask a specific perioperative question, such as whether known cardiomyopathy, valve disease, recent PCI or new rhythm change is clinically stable and whether any specific perioperative recommendations apply.

Communication With PCP

Use PCP involvement when the issue requires longer-term chronic disease management, not simply to obtain a signature before surgery.

Examples

  • New diabetes requiring longitudinal management
  • Persistent uncontrolled hypertension
  • New chronic disease diagnosis
  • Unexplained constitutional symptoms
  • Long-term medication management outside perioperative scope

Common Preventable Cancellation Causes

Problem Prevention Strategy
Anticoagulant not held Identify early, provide written instruction, verify last dose 24–48 hr before surgery.
Recent PCI not recognized Ask specifically about stents and obtain cardiology records early.
Device plan missing Identify CIED during intake and obtain interrogation before DOS.
SGLT2 hold missed Flag medication during reconciliation and verify multi-day hold.
GLP-1 aspiration concern Ask about dose escalation and GI symptoms before DOS.
Insulin plan missing Provide written day-of-surgery insulin instructions.
Abnormal lab not reviewed Route significant abnormalities immediately and track repeat testing.
Recent hospitalization discovered on DOS Ask about interval ER/hospital visits at final readiness check.
Specialist note never received Track requested records with owner and due date.
Active infection Complete symptom/wound screen before final optimization.

Medication Instruction Communication

Patients should receive medication instructions in a format they can understand and reference later.

Instructions should specify:

  • Medication name
  • Exact last dose when relevant
  • How many days/hours to hold
  • What to take the morning of surgery
  • Insulin dose adjustment
  • Who to call with questions

Teach-Back

Medication teach-back:

“What day will you take your last Eliquis dose?”

“What will you do with your insulin the morning of surgery?”

“When was your last Jardiance dose?”

“What symptoms should you call us about before surgery?”
Teach-back is more reliable than simply asking, “Do you understand?”

Escalation Timing

Time Before Surgery Priority
>1 week Obtain specialist records, cardiac/device testing, biologic plans, anemia workup.
3–7 days Verify medication holds, review outstanding testing, resolve anesthesia questions.
24–48 hours Close pending items, verify last doses, screen for interval health change.
Day of surgery Only true interval changes should remain—not unresolved known issues.

Do Not Over-Escalate

Efficient communication also means avoiding unnecessary referrals that create delay without improving patient safety.

Examples

  • Stable chronic RBBB without symptoms
  • Mild stable anemia before minimal-blood-loss surgery
  • Stable controlled hypertension
  • Asymptomatic bacteriuria
  • Stable CKD without acute change
  • Stable COPD without exacerbation

Do Not Under-Escalate

Do not leave unresolved:

New chest pain
Decompensated HF
New LBBB without appropriate pathway review
Recent ICD shock
Unknown anticoagulant last dose
Recent PCI with uncertain antiplatelet plan
24-hour oxygen dependence without venue review
DKA/HHS concern
Active systemic infection
Progressive AKI
Major unexplained anemia before high-blood-loss surgery

Key Takeaways

  • Every unresolved issue needs an owner.
  • Use specific clinical questions rather than vague “review” requests.
  • Record retrieval is not complete until the relevant report is reviewed.
  • Teach-back improves medication-instruction reliability.
  • Anticoagulant and diabetes medication problems should be identified days before surgery.
  • Recent hospitalization should be screened again before DOS.
  • Do not automatically seek “clearance” when the preoperative provider can resolve the issue.
  • Do escalate unstable disease or questions that require specialty expertise.
  • A daily pending-items huddle can prevent avoidable cancellations.
  • Review cancelled cases for process failures and recurring themes.
  • The goal is safe surgery with fewer unnecessary delays—not simply fewer cancellations.
