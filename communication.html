Preoperative Communication, Collaboration & Same-Day Cancellation Prevention

Team-based strategies for provider, RN, anesthesia, surgeon and ancillary staff

Home Evaluation SPA Scope Testing Windows Surgical Risk

Purpose

Effective preoperative communication reduces duplication, missed testing, unclear medication instructions, unresolved specialty recommendations and preventable same-day cancellations.

The goal is to identify every clinically important issue early, assign an owner, define the next action and confirm resolution before the patient arrives for surgery.

Core Team Members

Team Member Primary Preoperative Role
SPA Provider Medical evaluation, interpretation, risk stratification, optimization, escalation and final optimization status.
Preoperative RN Intake, medication reconciliation, record collection, screening, standing-order tasks and escalation of abnormal findings.
Anesthesia Airway, aspiration, anesthetic technique, neuraxial safety, device, severe pulmonary and anesthesia-specific risk decisions.
Surgeon Procedure-specific bleeding risk, timing, expected blood loss, postoperative medication resumption and benefit-risk discussion.
PCP / Specialist Longitudinal disease management and specialty-specific recommendations when required.
Ancillary Staff Scheduling, records, laboratory coordination, respiratory therapy, pharmacy, PT/OT and discharge support.

Single Source of Truth

Every patient should have one clearly documented preoperative status that reflects the most current information. Avoid parallel plans in separate notes that conflict with each other.

When new information changes the plan, update the primary preoperative note or tracking system and communicate the change to the relevant team members.

High-Risk Communication Failures

Common causes of preventable cancellation include:
  • Anticoagulant or antiplatelet hold not clarified
  • Recent cardiac event not identified
  • New abnormal EKG not reviewed
  • Abnormal potassium or creatinine not repeated
  • SGLT2 inhibitor not held appropriately
  • GLP-1 medication risk not reviewed
  • Insulin plan not established
  • OSA/PAP status not communicated
  • Device interrogation unavailable
  • Recent stroke/TIA timing unresolved
  • Active infection discovered on surgery day
  • Specialist records requested but never reviewed
  • Pending test had no assigned owner

Use Closed-Loop Communication

Closed-loop communication means that the concern is sent, received, acknowledged and resolved.

Closed-loop sequence:

1. Issue identified
2. Responsible person notified
3. Notification acknowledged
4. Action completed
5. Result reviewed
6. Final plan documented
7. Relevant team members informed

SBAR for Preoperative Escalation

S – Situation
What is the immediate concern?

B – Background
What relevant history or testing supports the concern?

A – Assessment
What is the current clinical interpretation?

R – Recommendation
What specific action or decision is needed?

SBAR Example: New LBBB

Situation: New LBBB identified on preoperative EKG.

Background: Patient scheduled for elective lumbar fusion. No prior LBBB documented on available EKG. Patient denies active chest pain but has limited functional capacity.

Assessment: New conduction abnormality requires further cardiovascular risk assessment under institutional pathway.

Recommendation: Hold final optimization status as Pending and obtain cardiology evaluation or appropriate pharmacologic nuclear stress assessment before elective surgery.

SBAR Example: Anticoagulant Hold Unclear

Situation: Patient taking apixaban with no documented hold instructions.

Background: Medication is prescribed for atrial fibrillation. Procedure involves spine surgery with high bleeding consequence.

Assessment: Surgery cannot safely proceed without confirmed interruption and resumption plan.

Recommendation: Obtain prescribing-provider/anesthesia/ surgeon guidance and document exact last dose and postoperative resumption plan.

Communication With Anesthesia

Contact anesthesia early when the issue relates directly to anesthetic safety rather than waiting until the day of surgery.

High-Yield Reasons

  • Difficult airway history
  • Severe OSA with PAP intolerance
  • GLP-1 therapy with significant GI symptoms
  • Potential aspiration risk
  • Severe pulmonary disease
  • Neuraxial anticoagulation question
  • Recent stroke timing concern
  • Pacemaker/ICD management question
  • History of malignant hyperthermia
  • Major prior anesthesia complication

Communication With the Surgeon

The surgeon should be involved when the medical issue changes procedural timing, bleeding risk, postoperative medication resumption or surgical venue.

Examples

  • New medical reason to postpone surgery
  • Significant anemia before expected high blood loss
  • Antiplatelet interruption question
  • Timing after stroke or cardiac event
  • Need for higher level of care
  • Active infection near surgery date
  • Postoperative clopidogrel or anticoagulant resumption

Communication With PCP

PCP communication should clearly distinguish what must be resolved before surgery from what requires longer-term follow-up.

Avoid vague messages such as “needs medical clearance.” State the specific abnormality and the exact question that needs to be addressed.

Better Questions

  • Is this creatinine change acute or chronic?
  • Has this anemia been previously evaluated?
  • Is this hypertension currently being treated or recently adjusted?
  • Is there an established plan for this newly identified diabetes?

Communication With Specialists

Specialist communication is most effective when the preoperative team asks a focused question.

Specialty Focused Question Example
Cardiology Does the new LBBB require additional ischemic evaluation before this intermediate-risk elective surgery?
Electrophysiology Is device reprogramming or magnet management needed for the planned use of electrocautery?
Pulmonology Is this patient with FEV1 below 50% appropriate for the planned facility and postoperative monitoring level?
Neurology Are there unresolved neurologic concerns affecting timing after the recent cerebrovascular event?
Hematology What perioperative plan is recommended for this severe thrombocytopenia or complex anticoagulation issue?

48–72 Hour Pre-Surgery Review

Patients with significant risk factors or unresolved items should undergo a final review before the day of surgery.

Confirm

  • Final optimization status
  • All required labs reviewed
  • All specialist records received
  • Anticoagulant/antiplatelet holds confirmed
  • SGLT2 instructions confirmed
  • GLP-1/anesthesia plan confirmed when relevant
  • Insulin instructions clear
  • PAP/device instructions clear
  • No new illness or hospitalization

Day-Before Patient Check

A brief patient-facing confirmation can identify interval changes that would otherwise appear on the day of surgery.

Ask:

Any new fever or infection?
New cough, wheezing or shortness of breath?
New chest pain?
ER visit or hospitalization since preop evaluation?
New medication?
Anticoagulant/antiplatelet hold completed?
SGLT2 hold completed?
GLP-1 instructions followed?
Any new neurologic symptom?
Any change in oxygen or PAP use?

Day-of-Surgery Escalation

Day-of-surgery findings that require immediate team discussion include:
  • Medication hold not followed
  • New fever or infection
  • Unexpected hypoxemia
  • New chest pain
  • New arrhythmia
  • New focal neurologic symptoms
  • Severe uncontrolled blood pressure
  • Significant hyperglycemia or hypoglycemia
  • Critical electrolyte abnormality
  • New aspiration concern

Do Not Hide Uncertainty

If a required issue is unresolved, document the status as Pending rather than creating false certainty. Clear documentation of uncertainty is safer than an unsupported final recommendation.

Optimization Status Communication

Status Team Meaning
Optimized No known unresolved medical issue currently prevents proceeding under the planned pathway.
Pending A specific item must be completed or reviewed before final determination.
Not optimized A significant unresolved medical issue currently makes proceeding inappropriate or outside facility criteria.

What a Good Pending Note Looks Like

FINAL PREOPERATIVE OPTIMIZATION STATUS: Pending.

Reason: New LBBB identified on preoperative EKG. Cardiology evaluation or appropriate ischemic assessment required under TSJH perioperative pathway. Cardiology records requested. RN assigned to confirm receipt and route results for provider review before final optimization determination.

What a Poor Pending Note Looks Like

Avoid documentation such as:

“Need clearance.”
“Waiting on cardiology.”
“Abnormal labs.”
“Will follow.”

These statements do not identify the exact issue, responsible person, required action or deadline.

Same-Day Cancellation Prevention Bundle

The highest-yield prevention bundle includes:
  • Early medication review
  • Early identification of recent cardiac/stroke events
  • Structured abnormal-lab follow-up
  • Focused specialist questions
  • Assigned ownership of pending items
  • 48–72 hour high-risk case review
  • Day-before interval-change screening
  • Clear Optimized / Pending / Not optimized status

Key Takeaways

  • Use one current preoperative plan as the source of truth.
  • Use closed-loop communication for every clinically important pending item.
  • Ask focused questions instead of requesting vague “clearance.”
  • Assign every pending item an owner and deadline.
  • Escalate anesthesia-specific concerns directly to anesthesia.
  • Include the surgeon when timing, bleeding risk or surgical venue changes.
  • Review high-risk cases before the day of surgery.
  • Screen for interval illness shortly before surgery.
  • Use Pending when the decision is genuinely unresolved.
  • Clear documentation and reliable follow-up reduce preventable same-day cancellations.
