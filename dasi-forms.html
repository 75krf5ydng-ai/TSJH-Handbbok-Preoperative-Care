Purpose

The Duke Activity Status Index (DASI) is a structured patient-reported tool used to estimate functional capacity before surgery. It is more consistent than simply asking the patient whether they can perform “4 METs.”

Ask the patient whether they can currently perform each activity without assistance because of cardiopulmonary or general physical limitation. Score only activities the patient reports they are able to perform.

Scoring

Add the assigned weight for every activity the patient answers YES. Activities answered NO receive zero points.

Maximum DASI score: approximately 58.2
Higher scores represent better functional capacity. Lower scores indicate progressively greater functional limitation.

DASI and Estimated Functional Capacity

DASI can be converted to an estimated peak oxygen consumption and functional capacity when needed.

Estimated peak VO₂:

VO₂ peak ≈ (0.43 × DASI score) + 9.6
Approximate METs:

Estimated METs ≈ VO₂ peak ÷ 3.5
For routine TSJH preoperative documentation, the preferred approach is to record the patient's numeric DASI score rather than filling the H&P with additional MET calculations unless clinically useful.

Functional Capacity and Cardiac Risk

Functional capacity should be incorporated into the overall perioperative cardiovascular assessment together with surgical risk, RCRI, symptoms, known cardiovascular disease and biomarkers when indicated.

A low DASI score does not automatically mean the patient needs a stress test. Additional testing should be ordered only when it is appropriate for the patient's overall risk and the result is expected to change management.

Important Orthopedic Limitation

A spine or joint patient may report poor functional capacity because pain, weakness or mechanical limitation prevents activity—not because of cardiopulmonary disease. Document this distinction when clinically relevant.

For example, a patient awaiting total knee arthroplasty may be unable to climb stairs because of severe knee pain despite having no exertional chest pain or cardiopulmonary limitation.

Provider Follow-Up Questions

When the DASI score is low, determine why the patient is unable to perform the activities.

Possible Limitation Clinical Significance
Joint or spine pain May underestimate cardiopulmonary reserve.
Neurologic weakness May reflect the surgical diagnosis rather than cardiac disease.
Dyspnea May represent cardiac or pulmonary limitation and deserves further assessment.
Chest discomfort Requires cardiovascular assessment.
General frailty Consider CFS and overall physiologic reserve.
Sedentary lifestyle Functional capacity may remain uncertain despite absence of symptoms.

Patient Instructions

Patients should answer based on what they are currently able to do—not what they could do years ago or what they believe they could do if their surgical problem were absent.

If the patient has never attempted an activity or is uncertain, the provider should clarify the reason rather than automatically assuming full functional capacity.

Key Takeaways

  • Use DASI instead of relying solely on an informal MET estimate.
  • DASI is patient reported and should reflect current ability.
  • Add the weights for all activities the patient can perform.
  • Higher scores reflect better functional capacity.
  • Document the numeric DASI score in the preoperative H&P.
  • A low score does not automatically mandate stress testing.
  • Determine whether orthopedic or neurologic disease is causing the functional limitation.
  • Exertional dyspnea or chest symptoms deserve separate clinical assessment.
