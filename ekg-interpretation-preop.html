Purpose

Preoperative EKG interpretation should determine whether findings are chronic or new, whether they reflect active cardiovascular disease and whether additional evaluation would change perioperative management.

The most useful preoperative EKG comparison is often the prior tracing. A stable chronic abnormality may require no additional workup, while a new change can be clinically significant.

Normal Sinus Rhythm

Normal sinus rhythm without clinically significant new abnormality generally requires no additional cardiovascular evaluation solely because elective noncardiac surgery is planned.

Sinus Tachycardia

Sinus tachycardia is a finding, not a diagnosis.

Consider

  • Pain
  • Anxiety
  • Dehydration
  • Anemia
  • Fever/infection
  • Hyperthyroidism
  • Hypoxemia
  • PE
  • Medication/stimulant effect
  • Withdrawal
Persistent unexplained tachycardia should be evaluated before elective surgery rather than dismissed as anxiety without clinical review.

Sinus Bradycardia

Asymptomatic sinus bradycardia may be normal in selected patients or medication related.
Symptomatic bradycardia, hemodynamic instability or significant conduction disease requires further evaluation.

First-Degree AV Block

Stable asymptomatic first-degree AV block generally does not require additional cardiology evaluation solely because surgery is planned.
Review medications and prior EKG when PR prolongation is marked or new.

Mobitz I / Wenckebach

Mobitz I may be benign in selected asymptomatic patients but should be interpreted with symptoms, medications and prior tracings.
New, symptomatic or unexplained second-degree block warrants further review.

Mobitz II

Mobitz II is clinically important conduction disease and generally requires cardiology/EP evaluation before elective surgery.

Complete Heart Block

Complete heart block requires urgent cardiovascular evaluation and is not a routine elective-surgery finding.

Right Bundle Branch Block

A stable chronic isolated RBBB in an asymptomatic patient often does not require additional testing solely for elective surgery.
Compare with prior EKG and assess for symptoms or additional conduction disease.

Left Bundle Branch Block

Under the TSJH perioperative pathway, LBBB requires adequate cardiovascular evaluation before elective surgery.

Why Exercise EKG Alone Is Limited With LBBB

Baseline LBBB can make exercise EKG ischemia interpretation unreliable. When ischemia assessment is needed, the TSJH pathway favors pharmacologic nuclear stress testing or cardiology evaluation.

Bifascicular Block

Examples include RBBB plus left anterior fascicular block or left posterior fascicular block.

Stable asymptomatic bifascicular block may not require intervention, but review for syncope, PR prolongation and prior conduction history.
Syncope or evidence of advanced conduction disease warrants cardiology/EP review.

Premature Atrial Contractions

Occasional PACs are common and generally do not require additional preoperative testing in an asymptomatic patient.
Frequent ectopy may warrant review of electrolytes, stimulants, thyroid disease or underlying atrial arrhythmia when clinically relevant.

Premature Ventricular Contractions

Occasional isolated PVCs without symptoms or known significant structural heart disease often do not require additional testing.
Frequent PVCs, couplets, NSVT, syncope or known cardiomyopathy may require additional evaluation.

Stable chronic rate-controlled AF with an established anticoagulation plan does not automatically require additional cardiac testing.
New AF, AF with RVR, hemodynamic instability or unexplained symptoms requires further evaluation.

Atrial Flutter

Interpret atrial flutter similarly to AF with respect to ventricular rate, symptoms, anticoagulation and hemodynamic stability.

SVT

Active sustained symptomatic SVT should be treated before elective surgery.
Remote controlled SVT without current symptoms may not require new testing if the diagnosis and management are established.

Ventricular Tachycardia

Sustained VT or clinically significant ventricular arrhythmia is an active cardiac issue requiring urgent cardiovascular evaluation.

QTc Interpretation

QTc should be interpreted with sex, heart rate, medications and electrolyte status.

Marked QTc prolongation increases torsades risk, particularly when combined with hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, bradycardia or multiple QT-prolonging drugs.

Review

  • Potassium
  • Magnesium
  • Calcium
  • Antidepressants
  • Antiarrhythmics
  • Antiemetics
  • Antipsychotics
  • Methadone
  • Other QT-prolonging medications

ST Elevation

New ST elevation associated with ischemic symptoms or a concerning acute pattern requires urgent evaluation for myocardial infarction.
Not all ST elevation is acute MI. Prior tracing, clinical symptoms and morphology matter.

ST Depression

New horizontal/downsloping ST depression may suggest ischemia, particularly with compatible symptoms.

New ischemic ST changes require clinical cardiovascular assessment before elective surgery.

Nonspecific ST-T Abnormality

Stable nonspecific ST/T-wave abnormalities are common and often do not require additional evaluation in an asymptomatic patient.
Always compare with the prior tracing when available.

T-Wave Inversion

T-wave inversion may reflect ischemia, strain, bundle branch block, CNS disease, normal variation or other conditions.

Interpret according to lead distribution, prior comparison and symptoms.
New deep symmetric T-wave inversion in a clinically compatible setting deserves further cardiovascular evaluation.

Pathologic Q Waves

Pathologic Q waves may indicate prior infarction. Remote stable Q waves without new symptoms do not automatically require stress testing.

Review known CAD history, prior imaging and functional status.

LVH

EKG criteria for LVH may reflect chronic hypertension or structural heart disease but do not independently require preoperative echocardiography.
Consider echo when there is a new murmur, heart-failure symptoms, suspected significant valve disease or another clinical indication.

Low Voltage

Low voltage can occur with obesity, COPD, pericardial effusion, infiltrative disease and other conditions.

Stable low voltage in an otherwise clinically stable patient may not require additional preoperative evaluation.

Poor R-Wave Progression

Poor R-wave progression is nonspecific and can reflect lead placement, prior anterior infarction, LVH or normal variation.

Compare prior EKG before escalating solely on this automated interpretation.

Automated EKG Interpretations

Do not rely solely on the machine-generated interpretation.

Automated reports may overcall infarction, ischemia, chamber enlargement and conduction disease. Review the tracing and compare prior studies when possible.

These examples assume the patient is clinically stable and there is no new symptom or relevant interval change.

Symptoms Override the EKG Label

Escalate regardless of a “nonspecific” EKG report when the patient has:

Active chest pain
New exertional pressure
New dyspnea concerning for cardiac disease
Syncope
Decompensated heart failure
Hemodynamic instability
Other active cardiac symptoms

EKG + Functional Capacity

A stable abnormal EKG in a patient with good functional capacity and no active cardiac symptoms may not require additional ischemic testing.

Use DASI, surgical risk, RCRI and active-cardiac-problem assessment together.

EKG + BNP

BNP/NT-proBNP screening and EKG interpretation answer different questions. A stable EKG does not eliminate protocol-directed BNP screening when indicated.
BNP ≥92 pg/mL or NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L triggers the applicable TSJH postoperative cardiac-surveillance protocol.

When Echocardiography May Be More Appropriate Than Stress Testing

  • New/worsening heart-failure symptoms
  • New murmur concerning for significant valve disease
  • Known moderate/severe valve disease requiring surveillance
  • Suspected change in ventricular function
  • Pulmonary hypertension assessment
Stress testing and echocardiography answer different clinical questions.

When Stress Testing May Be Appropriate

Stress testing should be ordered when the result is expected to change management—not solely because an EKG is abnormal.

For LBBB under the TSJH pathway, when adequate prior assessment is unavailable, use pharmacologic nuclear stress testing such as Lexiscan/regadenoson or cardiology evaluation.

Key Takeaways

  • Compare the EKG with a prior tracing whenever possible.
  • Do not rely exclusively on the automated EKG interpretation.
  • Stable RBBB, first-degree AV block and nonspecific ST-T changes often require no additional workup.
  • New LBBB requires adequate cardiovascular evaluation under the TSJH pathway.
  • Mobitz II and complete heart block require cardiology/EP review.
  • Stable controlled chronic AF is different from new AF or AF with RVR.
  • Occasional PACs/PVCs are usually benign in an asymptomatic patient.
  • Marked QTc prolongation requires medication and electrolyte review.
  • New ischemic ST changes require clinical cardiovascular assessment.
  • Old Q waves do not automatically require repeat stress testing.
  • Symptoms and clinical stability matter more than an isolated EKG label.
  • Order additional testing only when it will answer a meaningful perioperative question.
