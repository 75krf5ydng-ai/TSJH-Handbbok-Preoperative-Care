Purpose of the Preoperative Evaluation

The purpose of the preoperative medical evaluation is to identify medical conditions that may increase perioperative risk, determine whether additional evaluation or optimization is indicated, and develop an individualized perioperative management plan.

Key Principle: The goal of preoperative assessment is risk identification and medical optimization—not simply "clearance" for surgery.

Core Preoperative Assessment

A comprehensive preoperative evaluation should include:

  • Planned procedure and surgical indication
  • Medical and surgical history
  • Previous anesthesia history and complications
  • Medication reconciliation
  • Allergies and adverse drug reactions
  • Cardiovascular assessment
  • Pulmonary assessment
  • Functional capacity assessment
  • Frailty assessment when appropriate
  • Obstructive sleep apnea screening
  • Review of laboratory and diagnostic testing
  • Assessment of perioperative medication management
  • Identification of conditions requiring additional evaluation or optimization

Functional & Risk Screening

Tool Purpose
DASI Self-reported functional capacity assessment.
RCRI Estimates risk of major perioperative cardiac complications.
STOP-BANG Screening for obstructive sleep apnea.
Clinical Frailty Scale Assesses frailty and physiologic reserve.

Cardiovascular Risk Assessment

Cardiovascular evaluation should follow a stepwise approach consistent with current perioperative cardiovascular guidelines. The evaluation should consider the urgency and risk of the planned procedure, active cardiovascular conditions, clinical risk factors, functional capacity, and whether additional testing would change perioperative management.

Active Cardiac Conditions

Elective surgery may require postponement when an unstable or significant cardiac condition is identified. Examples include:

  • Acute coronary syndrome
  • Decompensated heart failure
  • Unstable or clinically significant arrhythmias
  • Severe symptomatic valvular heart disease
Important: Additional cardiac testing should generally be obtained only when the result is expected to change perioperative management.

BNP / NT-proBNP Screening

Per institutional perioperative protocols, BNP or NT-proBNP may be appropriate for selected patients with increased cardiovascular risk.

When postoperative cardiac-surveillance criteria are met, the perioperative plan may include postoperative ECG and serial troponin surveillance according to institutional protocol.

Medical Optimization

Clinically significant abnormalities identified during the preoperative evaluation should be addressed before elective surgery whenever possible. Optimization may include treatment, medication adjustment, additional diagnostic evaluation, specialty consultation, or development of a perioperative monitoring plan.

Common Areas Requiring Optimization

  • Hypertension
  • Coronary artery disease
  • Heart failure
  • Valvular heart disease
  • Cardiac arrhythmias
  • Diabetes mellitus
  • Anemia
  • Renal dysfunction
  • Electrolyte abnormalities
  • COPD or uncontrolled respiratory disease
  • Obstructive sleep apnea
  • Anticoagulant and antiplatelet management
  • Active infection
  • Frailty or poor functional status

Medication Review

Medication reconciliation should identify medications that require continuation, temporary interruption, dose adjustment, or special perioperative planning.

Particular attention should be given to:

  • Anticoagulants
  • Antiplatelet agents
  • Diabetes medications
  • Insulin
  • Cardiovascular medications
  • Diuretics
  • Renally cleared medications
  • Medications affecting anesthesia or postoperative respiratory status

Postoperative Planning

Preoperative evaluation should anticipate postoperative needs rather than end with the decision to proceed with surgery.

The postoperative plan should consider:

  • Cardiac surveillance when indicated
  • Respiratory monitoring
  • PAP therapy for patients with OSA when applicable
  • Supplemental oxygen or continuous pulse oximetry when indicated
  • Glucose monitoring
  • Renal function and electrolyte monitoring
  • DVT prophylaxis
  • Early mobilization
  • Multimodal analgesia
  • Constipation and postoperative ileus prevention
  • Discharge planning

Final Preoperative Optimization Status

Every preoperative evaluation should conclude with a clearly documented optimization status.

Status Meaning
OPTIMIZED Medical evaluation is complete and no unresolved condition currently prevents proceeding with the planned elective procedure.
PENDING Additional information, testing, treatment, records, or consultation is required before final optimization status can be determined.
NOT OPTIMIZED A medical condition or unresolved risk requires additional management before proceeding with elective surgery.
Recommended Documentation:

Patient meets TSJH perioperative consensus guidelines to proceed with elective surgery on scheduled surgery date at this time.

Documentation Standard

The preoperative note should clearly communicate the patient's medical risk, identified abnormalities, interventions performed, outstanding requirements, and postoperative recommendations to the surgeon, anesthesia team, nursing staff, and other members of the perioperative care team.
