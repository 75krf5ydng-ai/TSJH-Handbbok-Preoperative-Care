Purpose

The TSJH Preoperative Optimization Handbook is designed to support consistent, efficient and clinically appropriate preoperative evaluation for elective noncardiac surgery. It brings together TSJH institutional criteria, current professional guidance, validated risk tools, medication guidance, surveillance pathways and standardized documentation resources in one point-of-care resource.

Clinical decision support: This handbook does not replace physician, anesthesia, surgeon or specialty judgment. Current TSJH policy and applicable facility requirements remain the primary source for institutional eligibility and higher-level-of-care decisions.

Fastest Workflow

1. Start with the Core Preoperative Evaluation.
Review the planned procedure, medical history, active symptoms, recent hospitalization, functional capacity and major perioperative risk domains.
2. Enter the standardized risk screens.
Use DASI, RCRI, STOP-BANG and Clinical Frailty Scale as applicable. Use the handbook's calculators when additional structured risk assessment is needed.
3. Apply TSJH institutional criteria.
Check cardiac, pulmonary, medication, laboratory, infection, surveillance, device and higher-level-of-care pathways.
4. Resolve meaningful abnormalities.
Address active symptoms, abnormal testing, medication interruption, specialist recommendations, unresolved surveillance, anesthesia concerns and postoperative monitoring requirements.
5. Complete the Final 24–48 Hour Readiness review.
Confirm there has been no clinically meaningful interval change and that required plans, records, medication holds and monitoring arrangements are complete.
6. Assign the final optimization status.
Use only Optimized, Pending, or Not optimized.

Final Optimization Status

OPTIMIZED

Applicable perioperative requirements are satisfied and no clinically meaningful unresolved issue remains.

PENDING

A clinically meaningful item remains unresolved and requires completion, confirmation or review before final optimization.

NOT OPTIMIZED

An active or unstable medical issue requires treatment, additional evaluation, or an alternative perioperative plan before routine elective surgery.

TSJH documentation statement:

Patient meets TSJH perioperative consensus guidelines to proceed with elective surgery on scheduled surgery date at this time.
Avoid the phrase “cleared for surgery.” Document the patient's current optimization status, remaining issues and required perioperative plan.

What the Interactive Tools Do

Interactive tools are intended to organize clinical information and identify potential review or escalation needs. They do not independently determine whether a patient should undergo surgery.

  • Core Preoperative Evaluation: organizes the overall assessment and final status.
  • Cardiac tools: evaluate functional capacity, cardiac risk, BNP/NT-proBNP pathways, valve disease, heart failure, LBBB and pulmonary hypertension.
  • Pulmonary tools: incorporate STOP-BANG, CAT, BODE, Arozullah, oxygen use, pulmonary disease and postoperative respiratory planning.
  • Medication tools: provide TSJH quick references plus broader guideline context when applicable.
  • Laboratory and renal tools: identify meaningful abnormalities, trends and unresolved metabolic concerns.
  • Surveillance tools: determine whether existing imaging or specialty follow-up remains clinically usable.
  • Final Readiness: confirms that important unresolved items have been closed before the scheduled procedure.

How to Use TSJH Guidance vs National Recommendations

The handbook intentionally displays both institutional and broader professional guidance in selected areas.

TSJH quick reference / TSJH pathway
This identifies the facility-specific rule or working institutional standard.
Current guideline-based recommendation
This provides additional national or multisociety context when timing, testing or management may vary based on clinical circumstances.

When the recommendations differ, do not silently substitute one for the other. Identify the applicable TSJH requirement and incorporate anesthesia, surgeon, cardiology, pulmonary, vascular or other specialty recommendations when appropriate.

When to Escalate

Escalation should occur when a finding could materially affect anesthesia, surgical venue, bleeding risk, cardiopulmonary stability, postoperative monitoring or the safety of proceeding with elective surgery.

Examples include:

  • Active or unstable cardiac symptoms
  • Severe valve disease or unresolved significant structural heart disease
  • Heart-failure instability or TSJH higher-level-of-care criteria
  • Pulmonary hypertension or right-heart red flags
  • Significant pulmonary disease or chronic respiratory failure
  • Recent PCI/stent or required DAPT with unresolved interruption plan
  • Unresolved anticoagulation or neuraxial timing
  • Acute kidney injury or major electrolyte abnormality
  • Active infection or clinically important wound concern
  • Unresolved surveillance imaging or specialist follow-up
  • Clinically important frailty, mobility or disposition concern
  • Any meaningful interval change immediately before surgery

Recommended Navigation

For a routine preoperative evaluation, the following sequence is usually the fastest:

  1. Core Preoperative Evaluation
  2. Relevant cardiac/pulmonary/laboratory/medication chapter
  3. Clinical Calculators Resource Center when needed
  4. Surveillance Imaging & Follow-Up when prior studies are relevant
  5. Provider Optimization Workflow
  6. Final 24–48 Hour Readiness
  7. Final documentation and optimization status

Documentation Principles

  • Document clinically significant findings rather than copying every normal result.
  • Identify abnormal tests and explain the perioperative implication.
  • Clearly distinguish completed recommendations from pending recommendations.
  • Document medication interruption and restart planning when clinically relevant.
  • Include postoperative cardiac or respiratory surveillance when indicated.
  • State the final optimization status clearly.
  • Avoid ambiguous language such as “cleared for surgery.”

The Master Preoperative H&P and workflow tools are designed for TruBridge-friendly documentation and can be used to standardize note structure across the preoperative service.

Who May Benefit From This Handbook

This resource may be useful for preoperative advanced practice providers, physicians, anesthesia clinicians, registered nurses, surgical teams, quality leaders and other clinicians involved in preparing patients for elective surgery.

The goal is not to replace specialty expertise. The goal is to make the routine preoperative process more consistent, easier to navigate and less likely to miss a meaningful perioperative issue.

Keep the Handbook Current

Perioperative guidance changes over time. Medication recommendations, society guidelines and institutional policies should be reviewed periodically.

When TSJH policy changes, the relevant handbook page and interactive calculator should be updated together so static guidance and generated recommendations remain synchronized.
