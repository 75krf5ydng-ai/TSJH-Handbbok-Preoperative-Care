Preoperative Infection, Skin/Wound, Urinary & Implant-Surgery Screening

Distinguishing clinically important infection from incidental abnormal findings before elective spine and joint surgery

Home Evaluation Labs Medications Red Flags Resources

Purpose

The goal of preoperative infection screening is to identify active infections that may increase perioperative morbidity or implant infection risk without unnecessarily delaying surgery because of incidental laboratory abnormalities.

The presence of bacteria, leukocytes or an abnormal screening test does not by itself establish clinically significant infection.
Active systemic infection, bacteremia, symptomatic UTI, pyelonephritis, pneumonia, significant cellulitis or an infected wound requires clinical evaluation before elective surgery.

Urinalysis — Start With Symptoms

Before interpreting the urinalysis, determine whether the patient actually has symptoms compatible with urinary tract infection.

Symptoms supporting possible UTI

  • Dysuria
  • New urinary frequency
  • New urgency
  • Suprapubic discomfort
  • Gross hematuria with urinary symptoms
  • Fever with urinary symptoms
  • Flank pain or CVA tenderness

Urinalysis Interpretation

Finding Meaning Preoperative Interpretation
Leukocyte esterase Suggests white blood cells in urine Does not independently diagnose UTI.
Nitrite Suggests nitrate-reducing bacteria Supports bacteriuria but should be interpreted with symptoms.
Pyuria WBCs in urine Can occur without symptomatic infection.
Bacteria Bacteria visualized/reported May represent bacteriuria or contamination.
Squamous epithelial cells Possible collection contamination Consider repeat clean-catch specimen if the result matters clinically.
Blood Hematuria Not synonymous with infection; evaluate in clinical context.
Protein Possible renal/urologic disease Not a diagnostic marker for UTI.

Asymptomatic Bacteriuria

In most patients undergoing elective nonurologic surgery, asymptomatic bacteriuria should not automatically trigger antibiotic treatment or surgical delay.

This distinction is particularly important in older adults, where bacteriuria and pyuria may occur without symptomatic urinary infection.

Exceptions exist, particularly pregnancy and selected invasive urologic procedures involving mucosal trauma. Follow the applicable specialty pathway.

Spine & Joint Implant Surgery

The presence of an orthopedic implant does not automatically convert asymptomatic bacteriuria into a symptomatic infection.

Evaluate the patient's symptoms and overall clinical picture rather than treating an isolated abnormal urinalysis solely because hardware will be implanted.

When a Urine Culture May Be Appropriate

  • Urinary symptoms compatible with UTI
  • Concern for pyelonephritis
  • Recurrent or complicated UTI history with current symptoms
  • Recent resistant organism with recurrent symptoms
  • Failure of recent treatment
  • Selected invasive urologic procedures
  • Other specific clinical indication
Routine reflex culture of every abnormal urinalysis can identify colonization that does not require treatment.

Urine Culture Interpretation

Result Interpretation
Single organism + compatible symptoms May represent symptomatic UTI; treat according to culture and clinical scenario.
Positive culture without symptoms Consider asymptomatic bacteriuria rather than automatically diagnosing UTI.
Mixed flora Frequently suggests contamination.
Low-count growth Interpret according to collection method, organism and symptoms.
Resistant organism If infection is clinically present, use susceptibility-directed therapy.

Symptomatic UTI

A patient with clinically significant symptomatic urinary infection should be evaluated and treated before elective implant surgery.

Timing of surgery after treatment should depend on infection severity, clinical response, procedure and facility/surgeon pathway.

Pyelonephritis

Concerning findings include:

Fever
Flank pain
CVA tenderness
Systemic illness
Nausea/vomiting
Leukocytosis
Sepsis physiology

Active pyelonephritis is incompatible with routine elective surgery until adequately treated and clinically resolved.

Minor Skin Findings

A small superficial abrasion, healed lesion or noninfected remote skin condition is different from active cellulitis or a draining wound.

Document location, appearance and whether it is near the operative field. Escalate uncertain findings to the surgeon when an implant is planned.

Recent Antibiotic Use

Antibiotic use alone does not determine whether surgery should proceed. Determine why the antibiotic was prescribed and whether the underlying condition has resolved.

Ask

  • What infection was being treated?
  • When did symptoms begin?
  • When was the last antibiotic dose?
  • Have symptoms resolved?
  • Was a culture obtained?
  • Was the organism resistant?
  • Was hospitalization required?

Respiratory Infection

Further evaluation is warranted for:

Fever
New productive cough
Purulent sputum
New hypoxemia
Dyspnea beyond baseline
Abnormal lung examination
Suspected pneumonia
Systemic illness

Upper Respiratory Symptoms

Mild isolated upper respiratory symptoms do not always require cancellation. Consider severity, fever, lower respiratory involvement, airway reactivity, procedure and anesthesia type.

Anesthesia review is particularly useful when symptoms are recent, progressive or associated with wheezing, productive cough or lower respiratory disease.

Pneumonia

Active pneumonia should be treated and clinically resolved before routine elective surgery. Residual pulmonary impairment should be considered when determining timing.

Dental Infection

Active dental abscess or significant odontogenic infection should be addressed before elective implant surgery.
Routine dental findings without active infection should not automatically be treated as systemic infection.

MRSA / MSSA Screening

Follow the surgeon and facility pathway for preoperative nasal screening and decolonization when applicable.

Colonization is different from active infection. A positive screening test may lead to decolonization and/or perioperative antibiotic adjustments rather than automatic cancellation.

Decolonization

Facility protocols may include nasal decolonization and chlorhexidine bathing for selected orthopedic or spine patients.

Use the current institutional protocol rather than creating an independent antibiotic/decolonization regimen.

Inflammatory Markers

ESR and CRP are nonspecific inflammatory markers.

An isolated elevation does not diagnose infection. Interpret inflammatory markers in conjunction with symptoms, examination, imaging, cultures and the clinical reason they were obtained.

Leukocytosis

An elevated WBC count can result from infection, inflammation, steroids, smoking, stress and other conditions.

Review the degree of elevation, differential, trend, symptoms and physical examination rather than cancelling surgery based on the WBC count alone.
Marked or unexplained leukocytosis associated with fever, systemic symptoms or other evidence of infection requires further evaluation.

Recent Sepsis or Bacteremia

Recent bacteremia or sepsis requires documentation of adequate treatment, clinical resolution and appropriate follow-up before elective implant surgery.

Determine the source, organism, treatment course, blood-culture clearance when relevant and whether infectious-disease follow-up was required.

Indwelling Devices

Consider infection risk associated with chronic urinary catheters, vascular access devices, dialysis access and other indwelling hardware.

Drainage, erythema, tenderness, malfunction or systemic symptoms involving an indwelling device require further evaluation.

Diabetic Foot Wounds

An active infected diabetic foot wound, osteomyelitis or significant ulceration requires deliberate review before elective implant surgery.

Document wound location, infection status, treatment, vascular status when relevant and whether specialty wound/podiatry care is ongoing.

Pressure Injuries

Not every pressure injury represents active infection, but significant open wounds may affect positioning, postoperative mobility and infection risk.

Escalate significant wounds to the surgeon and anesthesia team as appropriate.

For patients scheduled for arthroplasty, document recent intra-articular injection history when required by the surgeon's pathway.

Immunosuppression

Patients receiving biologics, JAK inhibitors, transplant immunosuppression, chemotherapy or significant chronic steroids may have altered infection risk.

Review medication timing, CBC when indicated, active infection symptoms and the specialist/surgeon perioperative medication plan.

Key Takeaways

  • Symptoms matter more than an isolated abnormal urinalysis.
  • Pyuria does not independently diagnose UTI.
  • Bacteriuria does not necessarily mean symptomatic infection.
  • Mixed-flora urine cultures frequently represent contamination.
  • Asymptomatic bacteriuria generally should not be treated solely because a patient is undergoing nonurologic orthopedic or spine surgery.
  • Pregnancy and selected invasive urologic procedures are important exceptions.
  • Active systemic infection should be treated before routine elective surgery.
  • Active cellulitis, abscess or draining wounds require review before implant surgery.
  • A positive MRSA screening test represents colonization unless clinical infection is present.
  • Leukocytosis and elevated inflammatory markers are nonspecific.
  • Always determine why a patient recently received antibiotics.
  • Recent bacteremia or sepsis requires careful resolution review.
  • Avoid unnecessary antibiotics for incidental preoperative laboratory findings.
