Purpose

Preoperative laboratory interpretation should focus on whether an abnormality is acute or chronic, clinically significant, likely to change perioperative management and relevant to the planned procedure.

Do not treat every value outside the laboratory reference range as a reason to delay surgery. The most useful questions are whether the result represents instability, whether it has changed from baseline and whether it creates a specific perioperative risk.

Hemoglobin / Hematocrit

Interpret hemoglobin according to chronicity, symptoms, underlying cause, cardiovascular reserve and expected surgical blood loss.
A hemoglobin value that may be acceptable before a minor hand procedure may require additional optimization before bilateral arthroplasty or multilevel revision spine fusion.

Use the dedicated anemia chapter and calculator when anemia is clinically meaningful.

Anemia Pattern by MCV

MCV Pattern Common Considerations
<80 fL Microcytic Iron deficiency, blood loss, thalassemia, selected inflammatory states.
80–100 fL Normocytic CKD, inflammation, acute blood loss, early iron deficiency, hemolysis.
>100 fL Macrocytic B12/folate deficiency, liver disease, alcohol, thyroid disease, medications, marrow disease.

Platelets

Platelet significance depends on the degree of abnormality and bleeding consequence of the planned procedure.

Mild isolated thrombocytosis is often reactive and does not automatically require delay when the patient is otherwise stable.
Significant thrombocytopenia requires careful review before spine, neuraxial, SCS/intrathecal and other high bleeding-consequence procedures.

Thrombocytosis

Mild thrombocytosis may occur with iron deficiency, inflammation, infection, recent surgery or other reactive conditions.

Review the trend and likely cause. Persistent marked or unexplained thrombocytosis may require additional evaluation.

White Blood Cell Count

Leukocytosis is nonspecific and may result from infection, inflammation, steroids, smoking, physiologic stress or other causes.
Marked or unexplained leukocytosis associated with fever, infectious symptoms, hemodynamic change or other clinical concern requires further evaluation.

Leukopenia / Neutropenia

Assess severity, chronicity, medications, chemotherapy/immunosuppression and infection risk.

Severe neutropenia or active febrile neutropenia is not a routine elective surgery scenario and warrants specialist review.

A single elevated creatinine should be compared with prior values whenever possible. Stable CKD and acute kidney injury have very different perioperative implications.

Acute Kidney Injury

Concern increases with:

New creatinine rise
Dehydration
Hypotension
Recent nephrotoxin exposure
Obstruction
Hyperkalemia
Metabolic acidosis
Decreased urine output
Volume overload

Progressive or clinically significant AKI should be addressed before routine elective surgery.

Stable CKD

Stable chronic kidney disease does not automatically require nephrology evaluation before elective surgery.

Review

  • Creatinine/eGFR trend
  • Potassium
  • Bicarbonate
  • Anemia
  • Volume status
  • Renally cleared medications
  • NSAID/nephrotoxin exposure

BUN

An isolated BUN elevation may reflect dehydration, GI blood loss, catabolic state, steroids or renal dysfunction.

Interpret BUN together with creatinine, volume status and clinical context.

Potassium

Clinically significant true hyperkalemia requires evaluation before elective surgery, particularly when associated with EKG changes, AKI or progressive rise.
Unexpected mild potassium elevation should be repeated when specimen hemolysis or collection artifact is possible.

Hypokalemia

Assess severity, symptoms, diuretic use, vomiting/diarrhea and magnesium.

Severe hypokalemia may increase arrhythmia risk and should be corrected before elective anesthesia.

Magnesium

Magnesium may be particularly relevant when there is significant hypokalemia, QT prolongation, arrhythmia or chronic diuretic use.

Hypokalemia can be difficult to correct if significant magnesium deficiency is also present.

Sodium

Mild chronic sodium abnormalities may be tolerated, but acute or severe disturbances require evaluation.

Assess

  • Severity
  • Acute vs chronic
  • Symptoms
  • Volume status
  • Diuretics
  • SIADH risk
  • Heart/liver/renal disease

Hyponatremia

Severe or symptomatic hyponatremia can cause neurologic complications and requires correction/evaluation before elective surgery.
Mild stable chronic hyponatremia should be interpreted in context rather than automatically cancelling surgery.

Hypernatremia

Hypernatremia often suggests water deficit or impaired free-water access.

Clinically significant hypernatremia should prompt assessment for dehydration, neurologic symptoms and underlying cause.

Bicarbonate / CO2

Serum bicarbonate may provide clues to metabolic acidosis, chronic hypercapnia or other acid-base abnormalities.
Unexplained low bicarbonate—especially with SGLT2 exposure, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain or tachypnea—should prompt evaluation for ketoacidosis or another anion-gap process.

Anion Gap

Anion Gap = Sodium – (Chloride + Bicarbonate)

Interpret the reported institutional value when available.

Potential causes of elevated gap

  • DKA
  • Euglycemic DKA
  • Lactic acidosis
  • Renal failure
  • Toxic ingestion
  • Other metabolic processes

Glucose

A mildly or moderately elevated glucose is not the same as acute metabolic instability.
Severe hyperglycemia with dehydration, ketones, acidosis, altered mental status or systemic illness requires evaluation before elective surgery.

A1c

A1c reflects chronic glycemic exposure over the preceding approximately 2–3 months.

Use the surgeon/facility threshold when one is established for elective implant surgery. Elevated A1c should be distinguished from DKA, HHS or other acute metabolic instability.

Hypoglycemia

Clinically significant hypoglycemia should be treated and the cause understood before anesthesia.

Calcium

Interpret total calcium with albumin when clinically necessary.

Mild asymptomatic calcium abnormalities may not materially affect surgery, while severe abnormalities can create neurologic or cardiac risk.

Liver Enzymes

Mild isolated AST/ALT elevation is common and does not automatically require surgical delay.

Review

  • Degree of elevation
  • Bilirubin
  • Albumin
  • INR
  • Platelets
  • Symptoms
  • Known liver disease
  • Trend

AST / ALT

Stable mild transaminase elevation with preserved synthetic function is different from acute hepatitis or hepatic failure.
Marked acute transaminase elevation, jaundice, encephalopathy or other evidence of acute hepatic decompensation requires further evaluation.

Bilirubin

Elevated bilirubin should be characterized as direct vs indirect when clinically important.

Indirect bilirubin elevation may occur with hemolysis or Gilbert syndrome. Direct hyperbilirubinemia may reflect cholestatic/hepatobiliary disease.

Albumin

Low albumin may reflect chronic illness, inflammation, liver disease, renal loss or malnutrition.

Albumin can provide useful information about frailty/nutritional risk but is not a stand-alone reason to cancel surgery.

Alkaline Phosphatase

Elevation may originate from hepatobiliary or bone sources.

Interpret with bilirubin, AST/ALT, symptoms and known bone disease.

PT / INR

Unexpected INR elevation should be understood before surgery, particularly for spine, neuraxial and other high bleeding-consequence procedures.

Consider

  • Warfarin use
  • Liver disease
  • Vitamin K deficiency
  • Medication interactions
  • Laboratory error
  • Other coagulopathy

PTT

An isolated prolonged PTT may reflect heparin exposure, lupus anticoagulant, factor deficiency or laboratory artifact.

Interpret unexpected prolongation according to bleeding history, medication use and magnitude of abnormality.

Coagulation Tests & DOACs

Normal PT/INR/PTT does not reliably prove absence of clinically relevant DOAC effect.

Use medication timing, renal function and the applicable anticoagulation protocol rather than relying solely on routine coagulation tests.

Urinalysis

Finding Interpretation
Leukocyte esterase Suggests pyuria; does not independently diagnose symptomatic UTI.
Nitrite Supports bacteriuria but requires clinical context.
Bacteria May represent bacteriuria or contamination.
Squamous cells May indicate poor specimen collection/contamination.
Blood Hematuria; not synonymous with infection.
Protein May suggest renal disease; not diagnostic of UTI.

Abnormal UA Without Symptoms

In most nonpregnant patients undergoing nonurologic elective surgery, an abnormal UA or asymptomatic bacteriuria does not automatically require antibiotic treatment or delay.
Pregnancy and selected invasive urologic procedures with mucosal trauma are important exceptions.

Urine Culture

Interpret culture results with symptoms, organism, colony count, collection quality and procedure type.
Mixed flora frequently indicates contamination and should not automatically be treated as a true infection.

BNP / NT-proBNP

Under the applicable TSJH perioperative pathway:

BNP ≥92 pg/mL
or
NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L

→ Postoperative EKG + daily troponins for 48–72 hours.

Troponin

Preoperative troponin should not be ordered routinely in every patient.

When elevated, interpret in the context of symptoms, EKG, renal function, prior values and whether there is concern for acute myocardial injury.

TSH

Routine TSH testing is not required in every surgical patient.

More useful when

  • Known thyroid disease with uncertain control
  • Medication nonadherence
  • New suggestive symptoms
  • Prior markedly abnormal values without follow-up

Severe Thyroid Abnormalities

Overt uncontrolled thyrotoxicosis, thyroid storm, myxedema coma or severe decompensated hypothyroidism requires treatment before routine elective surgery.

When to Repeat an Abnormal Lab

Repeat when:

Result is unexpected
Specimen artifact is possible
Clinical status changed
Value is near a critical procedural threshold
Trend matters to the decision
Treatment was initiated and response must be confirmed
Prior abnormality may have resolved

When Not to Repeat Automatically

Stable chronic abnormality with adequate recent comparison
Mild isolated deviation unlikely to change management
Known stable CKD with recent adequate renal labs
Known stable mild anemia before minimal-blood-loss surgery
Stable chronic mild transaminase elevation
Asymptomatic abnormal UA when repeat testing would not change management

Critical vs Clinically Important

A laboratory's “critical value” is a laboratory reporting threshold. A clinically important preoperative value depends on the patient, procedure and clinical context.
Always follow facility critical-value notification policies even when the clinical significance appears limited.

Procedure-Specific Laboratory Significance

Procedure Type Laboratory Issues With Greater Importance
Minor hand / superficial procedure Major instability matters; mild chronic abnormalities may have limited impact.
Joint arthroplasty Anemia, glucose/A1c, renal function, infection, anticoagulation.
Spine fusion Anemia, platelets/coags, renal function, electrolytes, anticoagulation.
SCS / intrathecal Platelets, anticoagulant/antiplatelet timing, infection.
Major revision spine Hemoglobin, platelets, renal function, electrolytes, albumin/nutrition, coagulation.

Key Takeaways

  • Compare abnormal laboratory values with prior results whenever possible.
  • Stable chronic abnormalities are different from acute deterioration.
  • Do not use one hemoglobin or platelet threshold for every procedure.
  • Repeat unexpected potassium elevation when hemolysis is possible.
  • Progressive AKI is more concerning than stable CKD.
  • Severe sodium or potassium abnormalities require evaluation.
  • Unexplained low bicarbonate after SGLT2 exposure should raise concern for ketoacidosis.
  • Elevated A1c does not equal acute metabolic instability.
  • Mild stable transaminase elevation does not automatically require delay.
  • Routine coagulation tests do not reliably measure DOAC effect.
  • Abnormal UA without symptoms does not automatically mean UTI.
  • Mixed-flora urine culture often reflects contamination.
  • BNP/NT-proBNP surveillance thresholds should trigger the established postoperative protocol.
  • Order repeat testing when it can change the perioperative decision.
