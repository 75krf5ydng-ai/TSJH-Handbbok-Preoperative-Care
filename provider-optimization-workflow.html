Provider Preoperative Optimization Workflow

Medical interpretation, direct optimization, escalation decisions and final perioperative status

Home Evaluation RN Workflow Red Flags Medications Resources

Purpose

The preoperative provider is responsible for integrating the patient's history, functional status, physical examination, laboratory data, EKG, prior testing, specialist records and procedure-specific risk into a practical perioperative medical plan.

The goal is not to obtain unnecessary “clearance” from multiple specialists. The goal is to identify what can be reasonably optimized directly, what requires additional evaluation, and whether the patient can proceed in the planned surgical setting.

Conditions the Provider Can Often Optimize Directly

Issue Potential Direct Management
Stable hypertension Review trend, adherence and perioperative medication plan.
Mild anemia Classify pattern, order focused workup and initiate appropriate treatment.
Iron deficiency Initiate iron replacement when appropriate and determine need for further source evaluation.
Stable CKD Review electrolytes, renal function, nephrotoxins and medication dosing.
Stable COPD/asthma Confirm inhaler regimen, absence of exacerbation and postoperative pulmonary plan.
OSA Assess PAP adherence/intolerance and establish respiratory monitoring recommendations.
Stable diabetes Review A1c/current glucose and establish perioperative medication instructions.
Stable hypothyroidism Confirm treatment and assess need for updated labs only when clinically indicated.
Constipation risk Plan bowel prevention when postoperative opioids are expected.
Frailty Integrate CFS into postoperative mobility/disposition planning.

Do Not Automatically Send Abnormal Labs to PCP

The provider should first determine severity, chronicity, clinical relevance and whether the abnormality can be reasonably evaluated and optimized in the preoperative clinic.

Examples often manageable directly

  • Mild stable anemia
  • Mild glucose elevation
  • Prediabetic A1c
  • Stable CKD
  • Mild thrombocytosis
  • Mild asymptomatic leukocytosis
  • Mild isolated electrolyte abnormality
  • Abnormal UA without urinary symptoms

When PCP Involvement Is Helpful

PCP involvement is most useful when the issue requires longitudinal management rather than a perioperative-only decision.

  • New chronic disease requiring ongoing treatment
  • Poorly controlled hypertension requiring longer-term titration
  • New diabetes requiring long-term management
  • Complex chronic medication management
  • Unexplained symptoms requiring broader outpatient workup

Step 15 — Medication Risk

Review medications that can create perioperative withdrawal, respiratory, bleeding, hemodynamic or metabolic risk.

High-priority categories

  • Anticoagulants
  • Antiplatelets
  • SGLT2 inhibitors
  • GLP-1 agents
  • Insulin
  • Chronic steroids
  • Beta blockers
  • Antiseizure medications
  • Parkinson medications
  • Benzodiazepines
  • Chronic opioids
  • Buprenorphine/methadone
  • Biologics/JAK inhibitors

Specific Consultation Questions

Avoid generic requests such as “please clear patient for surgery.”
Better example:

“Patient scheduled for elective L3-5 fusion on ___. Known ischemic cardiomyopathy with EF 40%. No CHF admission in 2 years, NYHA II, no current angina. Please assess current cardiovascular stability and provide any specific perioperative recommendations for planned noncardiac surgery.”

Postoperative BNP Protocol

When BNP ≥92 pg/mL or NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L under the applicable TSJH protocol, implement postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.

OSA Postoperative Plan

PAP anytime asleep when prescribed and tolerated. For PAP-intolerant OSA, continuous pulse oximetry is appropriate when indicated, with NIPPV and/or supplemental oxygen available when clinically warranted.

Final Decision — Optimized

Use Optimized when:

Required evaluation is complete
No active unstable medical condition is present
Clinically important abnormalities have been addressed
Medication plan is established
Required specialist/anesthesia recommendations are available
Postoperative monitoring plan is appropriate
Planned surgical venue is appropriate

Final Decision — Pending

Use Pending when a required item remains unresolved, such as:

Repeat laboratory testing
Cardiac testing
Device interrogation
Anticoagulation plan
Anesthesia review
Specialist recommendation
Resolution of symptomatic infection
Further anemia optimization
Other required clinical decision

Final Decision — Not Optimized

Use Not optimized when there is a current condition requiring treatment, stabilization, delay or higher-level-of-care planning before elective surgery.

Examples

  • Active ACS
  • Decompensated HF
  • Unstable arrhythmia
  • Active systemic infection
  • DKA/HHS
  • Acute pulmonary decompensation
  • Progressive significant AKI
  • Active major bleeding
  • Other acute instability

Key Takeaways

  • The provider should directly optimize conditions that reasonably fall within preoperative medical scope.
  • Do not automatically refer every abnormality to PCP or a specialist.
  • Use specialists for specific unresolved questions or unstable disease.
  • Use anesthesia for airway, anesthetic, neuraxial, device and venue-specific concerns.
  • Stable chronic abnormalities often do not require repeat testing.
  • Procedure risk and expected blood loss change the significance of medical findings.
  • Medication management is part of medical optimization.
  • Postoperative monitoring should be planned before surgery.
  • Every Pending status needs a reason and an owner.
  • Do not use the phrase “cleared for surgery.”
  • Final status should be Optimized, Pending or Not optimized.
