Cardiac Quick Reference

BNP Surveillance

BNP ≥92 pg/mL
or
NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L

→ Postoperative EKG + daily troponins for 48–72 hours under the applicable TSJH pathway.

HFrEF / Cardiomyopathy

EF <35% → higher-level-of-care concern.
For EF ≥35%, review symptoms, NYHA class, GDMT, cardiology follow-up, recent CHF admission history and current functional status.

Valve Disease Quick Reference

Aortic Stenosis

Severe AS may include:

AVA <1.0 cm²
Mean gradient >40 mmHg
Vmax >4.0 m/s
Severe or symptomatic AS requires cardiovascular review and may exceed routine TSJH pathway criteria.

Mitral Stenosis

Severe MS may include:

MVA ≤1.5 cm²
Mean gradient ≥10 mmHg
PASP >50 mmHg
Moderate-to-severe or symptomatic MS requires appropriate cardiovascular assessment.

LBBB

Under the TSJH pathway, LBBB requires adequate cardiovascular evaluation. When prior assessment is not sufficient, use pharmacologic nuclear stress testing such as Lexiscan/regadenoson or cardiology evaluation.

OSA Quick Plan

PAP anytime asleep when prescribed and tolerated. RT assistance as needed. Continuous pulse oximetry when indicated. For PAP-intolerant OSA, use NIPPV and/or supplemental oxygen when clinically warranted.

Diabetes Medication Quick Reference

SGLT2

Requires multi-day interruption before elective surgery because of euglycemic DKA risk.

Examples: Jardiance, Farxiga, Invokana, Steglatro.

GLP-1 / GIP

Review current dose, recent escalation, nausea, vomiting, bloating, early satiety and gastroparesis. Significant GI symptoms → anesthesia review.
Type 1 diabetes requires ongoing basal insulin during fasting. Do not tell a type 1 patient to hold all insulin.

Renal Quick Reference

Escalate for:

Progressive AKI
Significant hyperkalemia
Severe acidosis
Volume instability
Unresolved dialysis timing
Dialysis-access infection
Stable CKD alone does not automatically require nephrology evaluation before elective surgery.

Anemia Quick Reference

Pattern Think About
Microcytic Iron deficiency, chronic blood loss, thalassemia, inflammatory anemia
Normocytic CKD, inflammation, blood loss, early iron deficiency, hemolysis
Macrocytic B12, folate, liver disease, alcohol, thyroid, medications, marrow disorder
Ferritin <~30 ng/mL strongly supports iron deficiency in many settings. TSAT <~20% supports impaired iron availability when interpreted with ferritin and inflammation.
Do not use one universal hemoglobin cancellation threshold for every surgery. Consider symptoms, trend, cause, expected blood loss and cardiovascular reserve.

Infection Quick Reference

Abnormal UA without urinary symptoms ≠ symptomatic UTI.

Asymptomatic bacteriuria generally does not require treatment solely because elective nonurologic spine or joint surgery is planned.
Delay/reevaluate for:

Active bacteremia/sepsis
Pyelonephritis
Active pneumonia
Significant cellulitis
Draining/infected wound
Other active systemic infection
