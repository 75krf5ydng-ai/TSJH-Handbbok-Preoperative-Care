Final Preoperative Optimization Status

Optimized

No unresolved medical optimization issue, required testing, or specialty review preventing planned surgery.

Pending

Testing, records, specialist/anesthesia review, medication plan, repeat labs, or another optimization item remains unresolved.

Not Optimized

Active or unstable disease, hard stop, or higher-level-of-care issue is present.

Documentation

Avoid "cleared for surgery." State final optimization status, rationale, and recommended next step.

Cardiac Quick Reference

Surgical Risk

Low: <1%

Intermediate: 1–5%

High: >5%

Active Cardiac Problems

Active or unstable cardiac disease should not be labeled Optimized until appropriately evaluated and stabilized.

LBBB: cardiology evaluation or pharmacologic nuclear stress testing is used when ischemic evaluation is needed under the TSJH pathway.

BNP / NT-proBNP & Postoperative Surveillance

Screen When Appropriate

  • RCRI ≥1
  • Age ≥65 years
  • Age 45–64 with significant cardiac disease

Trigger Thresholds

  • NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L
  • BNP ≥92 pg/mL
If threshold is met: PACU EKG plus daily troponins for 48–72 hours under the applicable TSJH postoperative surveillance protocol.
If below threshold and there is no other indication, routine postoperative cardiac biomarker surveillance is generally not required solely on this basis.

Valve Disease

Aortic Stenosis

Severe AS criteria used in this handbook:

  • AVA <1.0 cm²
  • Mean gradient >40 mmHg
  • Vmax >4.0 m/s

Moderate/severe or symptomatic disease requires cardiovascular review. Severe AS is a higher-level-of-care concern.

Mitral Stenosis

  • MVA ≤1.5 cm²
  • Mean gradient ≥10 mmHg
  • PASP >50 mmHg

Moderate-to-severe disease requires cardiovascular review.

Heart Failure / Reduced EF

LVEF <35% is a TSJH higher-level-of-care trigger.

For patients with EF ≥35%, review symptom stability, NYHA class, recent HF admissions, cardiology assessment when indicated, guideline-directed therapy, functional capacity, and EKG for new ischemic changes.

Pulmonary Hypertension / Right Heart

TSJH higher-level-of-care red flags:
  • WHO Group 1 pulmonary hypertension
  • RVSP >65 mmHg
  • Severe tricuspid regurgitation
  • Moderate-to-severe RV dysfunction
  • Advanced pulmonary vasodilator therapy

OSA + PAP

Use PAP whenever asleep when prescribed; coordinate RT support as needed.

PAP Intolerance

Plan continuous oximetry and respiratory support/escalation as clinically indicated.

CVA / TIA Timing

Use current anesthesia/facility guidance and individualized review. When the interval is borderline, document multidisciplinary discussion rather than treating timing as an automatic clearance rule.

Aspirin

Aspirin management is procedure- and indication-specific. Continue when cardiovascular risk requires it unless the procedural bleeding risk or surgeon/anesthesia plan requires interruption. Spine, neuromodulation, and joint procedures may use procedure-specific hold intervals.

SGLT2 / GLP-1 / Diabetes

SGLT2 Inhibitors

Use current perioperative guidance to reduce euglycemic DKA risk. Assess ketones/anion gap when clinically indicated.

GLP-1 Therapy

Assess aspiration risk, GI symptoms, dosing schedule, and current anesthesia guidance rather than applying a single universal rule.

Insulin

Create a patient-specific perioperative insulin plan accounting for basal insulin, prandial insulin, pumps, NPO status, and expected glucose monitoring.

Hyperglycemia

Address marked current hyperglycemia independently of A1c. Evaluate DKA/HHS when clinically suspected.

Laboratory Interpretation Quick Reference

Hyponatremia

Review chronicity, glucose, volume status, medications, symptoms, and etiology. Avoid overly rapid correction of chronic hyponatremia.

Potassium

Repeat unexpected or hemolyzed values. Check magnesium and renal function. Significant abnormalities may require correction before elective surgery.

Anemia

Review CBC trend, MCV/RDW, iron studies, renal function, bleeding history, and expected surgical blood loss. Treat reversible causes when possible.

Renal Dysfunction

Compare creatinine/eGFR with baseline, assess volume status and nephrotoxins, and renally dose medications.

Platelets

Review severity, trend, bleeding/thrombotic history, iron deficiency, liver disease, medications, and procedure/anesthesia thresholds.

Liver / Coagulation

Interpret AST/ALT, bilirubin, albumin, INR, and synthetic function in clinical context rather than using an isolated value alone.

Common Perioperative Documentation Reminders

  • Use DASI rather than a generic METS statement when following the current handbook workflow.
  • Record STOP-BANG, CFS, and RCRI as numeric results.
  • State active cardiac problems as Yes/No with diagnosis when present.
  • Address abnormal labs and medication issues directly in the Assessment & Plan.
  • Use "Optimized," "Pending," or "Not optimized" for final status.
