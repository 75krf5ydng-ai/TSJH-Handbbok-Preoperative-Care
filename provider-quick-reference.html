Final Preoperative Status

Optimized

No unresolved medical optimization issue, required testing, or specialty review preventing planned surgery.

Pending

Testing, records, specialist/anesthesia review, medication plan, or another optimization item remains unresolved.

Not Optimized

Active or unstable disease, hard stop, or higher-level-of-care issue is present.

Wording

Avoid "cleared for surgery." State final optimization status, rationale, and recommendations.

Higher-Level-of-Care Red Flags

  • LVEF <35%
  • Severe aortic stenosis: AVA <1.0 cm², mean gradient >40 mmHg, or Vmax >4.0 m/s
  • WHO Group 1 pulmonary hypertension
  • RVSP >65 mmHg
  • Severe tricuspid regurgitation
  • Moderate-to-severe RV dysfunction
  • Advanced pulmonary vasodilator therapy

BNP / Troponin Surveillance

BNP screening when appropriate
  • RCRI ≥1
  • Age ≥65 years
  • Age 45–64 with significant cardiac disease
TSJH postoperative surveillance trigger
  • NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L
  • BNP ≥92 pg/mL
If threshold is met: postoperative EKG plus daily troponins for 48–72 hours under the applicable TSJH surveillance protocol.
If BNP/NT-proBNP is below threshold and there is no other indication, routine postoperative cardiac biomarker surveillance is generally not required.

Valve Disease

Aortic Stenosis

  • AVA <1.0 cm²
  • Mean gradient >40 mmHg
  • Vmax >4.0 m/s

Moderate/severe or symptomatic disease warrants cardiovascular review. Severe AS is a higher-level-of-care concern for TSJH.

Mitral Stenosis

  • MVA ≤1.5 cm²
  • Mean gradient ≥10 mmHg
  • PASP >50 mmHg

Moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis requires cardiovascular review.

Heart Failure / LVEF

LVEF <35% is a TSJH higher-level-of-care threshold.
For HFrEF patients considered for TSJH, confirm acceptable EF, recent cardiology review when required, stable symptoms, appropriate medical therapy, no recent decompensated HF, and no new ischemic findings.

Pulmonary Hypertension / Right Heart

Higher-level-of-care red flags include WHO Group 1 pulmonary hypertension, RVSP >65 mmHg, severe TR, moderate-to-severe RV dysfunction, or advanced pulmonary vasodilator therapy.

LBBB

New or relevant LBBB requires cardiology evaluation or appropriate pharmacologic nuclear stress testing rather than routine exercise ECG stress testing when ischemic evaluation is needed.

CVA / Neurologic Timing

For elective noncardiac surgery after CVA, apply anesthesia and facility timing guidance. When timing is borderline, document multidisciplinary discussion rather than treating the interval as an automatic clearance rule.

OSA / Pulmonary Quick Reference

OSA

  • Use PAP whenever asleep when prescribed.
  • Consider continuous oximetry when indicated.
  • Coordinate RT assistance when needed.

Pulmonary Red Flags

  • Continuous oxygen requirement
  • Severe obstructive disease / poor pulmonary reserve
  • Chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure
  • Advanced interstitial pulmonary disease

Aspirin Quick Reference

Aspirin management is procedure- and indication-specific. Continue when cardiovascular risk requires it unless the procedural bleeding risk or surgeon/anesthesia plan requires interruption. Spine, neuromodulation, and joint procedures may use procedure-specific hold intervals.

SGLT2 / GLP-1 / Diabetes

SGLT2 Inhibitors

Use current perioperative guidance to reduce euglycemic DKA risk. Assess for ketones/anion gap when clinically indicated.

GLP-1 Therapy

Assess aspiration risk, GI symptoms, dosing schedule, and current anesthesia guidance rather than applying a single universal rule.

Insulin

Create a patient-specific perioperative insulin plan. Account for basal insulin, prandial insulin, pumps, NPO status, and expected glucose monitoring.

Hyperglycemia

Marked current hyperglycemia should be addressed independently of A1c. Evaluate DKA/HHS when clinically suspected.

Common Abnormal Lab Reminders

Hyponatremia

Review chronicity, glucose, volume status, medications, and symptoms. Avoid overly rapid correction of chronic hyponatremia.

Potassium

Repeat unexpected or hemolyzed values. Check magnesium and renal function. Significant abnormalities may require correction before elective surgery.

Anemia

Review CBC trend, MCV/RDW, iron studies, renal function, bleeding history, and anticipated blood loss. Treat reversible causes when possible.

Renal Dysfunction

Compare creatinine/eGFR with baseline, assess volume status and nephrotoxins, and renally dose medications.
