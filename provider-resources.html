Cardiac Risk Quick Reference

Stepwise approach

1. Identify active or unstable cardiovascular disease.

2. Determine surgical risk.

3. Calculate RCRI when appropriate.

4. Assess functional capacity using DASI.

5. Determine whether BNP/NT-proBNP screening applies.

6. Review prior cardiac testing when relevant.

7. Order additional testing only when the result is expected to change management.

TSJH BNP / NT-proBNP Quick Reference

Biomarker Threshold Action
BNP ≥92 pg/mL Postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.
NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L Postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.
SmartPhrase

BNP screening. Obtain BNP because of age greater than 65 years and RCRI score of 1. If BNP is 92 pg/mL or greater, implement the TSJH postoperative cardiac-surveillance protocol with postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.

Aspirin Quick Reference

Situation General TSJH Approach
Joint surgery Commonly hold approximately 5 days when appropriate.
Spine surgery Commonly hold approximately 7 days when appropriate.
SCS / intrathecal procedure Follow high-bleeding-consequence procedural protocol.
Dual/triple antithrombotic therapy Do not independently interrupt aspirin without reviewing indication and specialty/procedural guidance.

Iron Deficiency Pattern

Typical findings may include:

Low hemoglobin
Low MCV or developing microcytosis
Low ferritin
Low serum iron
Increased TIBC
Low transferrin saturation
Do not assume all preoperative anemia is iron deficiency. Identify the likely etiology and consider expected surgical blood loss when determining whether additional optimization is needed.

OSA Quick Reference

Known OSA using PAP:
Bring PAP device when instructed and use PAP anytime asleep postoperatively.

OSA intolerant of PAP:
Document intolerance. Use continuous pulse oximetry postoperatively when indicated. NIPPV and/or supplemental oxygen may be required based on clinical status.

Valvular Disease Quick Reference

Aortic Stenosis

Severe aortic stenosis is generally outside routine TSJH elective surgical criteria.
Severe AS may include:

Aortic valve area <1.0 cm²
Mean gradient >40 mmHg
Peak velocity >4.0 m/s

Mitral Stenosis

Severe MS may include:

Mitral valve area ≤1.5 cm²
Mean gradient ≥10 mmHg
Pulmonary artery systolic pressure >50 mmHg

Heart Failure Quick Reference

EF <35% should prompt review for higher level of care.
For selected patients with ischemic cardiomyopathy/HFrEF, TSJH criteria may allow proceeding when EF is at least 35%, the patient is clinically stable, cardiology follow-up is current, functional status is acceptable, GDMT is optimized and there has been no recent heart-failure instability.

Pulmonary Hypertension Red Flags

Consider higher-level-of-care evaluation for:

WHO Group 1 pulmonary arterial hypertension
RVSP >65 mmHg
Severe tricuspid regurgitation
Moderate-to-severe RV dysfunction
Advanced pulmonary vasodilator therapy

Preoperative EKG Red Flags

Findings that may require additional evaluation include:

New ischemic ST/T-wave changes
New LBBB
High-grade AV block
Symptomatic bradycardia
Rapid uncontrolled atrial fibrillation
Significant new arrhythmia
Marked QT prolongation with contributing medication/electrolyte abnormalities
A new LBBB should prompt pharmacologic nuclear stress evaluation or cardiology assessment according to the TSJH pathway before elective surgery.

Stroke / TIA Timing

Recent cerebrovascular events require individualized assessment of neurologic, cardiovascular, anesthetic and procedural risk. Elective surgery timing should be reviewed with anesthesia and the appropriate treating clinicians when the event is recent.
