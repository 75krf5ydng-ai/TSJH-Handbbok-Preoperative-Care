Preoperative Pulmonary Evaluation & Optimization

Respiratory risk assessment for elective noncardiac surgery

Purpose

Preoperative pulmonary assessment identifies patients at increased risk for postoperative pulmonary complications and determines whether respiratory disease is sufficiently optimized for elective surgery.

Important complications include hypoxemia, bronchospasm, pneumonia, atelectasis, respiratory failure, prolonged mechanical ventilation, aspiration, and exacerbation of chronic lung disease.

The goal is not to eliminate all pulmonary risk. The goal is to identify clinically significant disease, optimize reversible conditions, determine whether additional testing or consultation is needed, and establish an appropriate perioperative respiratory plan.

Initial Pulmonary Assessment

Every preoperative evaluation should assess the patient's respiratory history, current symptoms, baseline oxygenation, smoking status, sleep apnea risk, functional status, recent respiratory infections, and known chronic pulmonary disease.

Evaluate Important Findings
Respiratory symptoms Dyspnea, wheezing, cough, sputum production, hemoptysis, orthopnea, chest congestion, or reduced exercise tolerance.
Chronic lung disease COPD, asthma, restrictive lung disease, pulmonary fibrosis, bronchiectasis, pulmonary hypertension, or chronic respiratory failure.
Sleep-disordered breathing Known OSA, PAP use, PAP intolerance, prior sleep study, or elevated STOP-BANG score.
Oxygen requirement Room-air saturation, nocturnal oxygen, exertional oxygen, or continuous home oxygen use.
Recent illness URI, influenza, COVID-19, pneumonia, COPD exacerbation, asthma exacerbation, fever, or recent antibiotic or steroid treatment.
Smoking Current use, pack-year history, cessation date, and vaping or other inhaled exposures.

Postoperative Pulmonary Complication Risk

Pulmonary risk is influenced by both patient-specific and procedure-specific factors.

Patient-Related Risk Factors

  • Advanced age
  • COPD
  • Uncontrolled asthma
  • Obstructive sleep apnea
  • Obesity hypoventilation syndrome
  • Chronic respiratory failure
  • Pulmonary hypertension
  • Active smoking
  • Recent respiratory infection
  • Poor functional capacity
  • Frailty
  • Neuromuscular weakness

Procedure-Related Risk Factors

  • Long operative duration
  • General anesthesia
  • Major spine surgery
  • Thoracic or upper abdominal surgery
  • Significant blood loss
  • Emergency surgery
  • Prolonged postoperative immobility

Arozullah Respiratory Failure Risk Index

The Arozullah Respiratory Failure Risk Index may be used to assist with estimation of postoperative respiratory-failure risk in selected surgical patients.

Documentation format:

Arozullah score: ___
An elevated respiratory-risk score should prompt review of the patient's pulmonary status, surgical complexity, anticipated anesthesia requirements, postoperative monitoring needs, and institutional suitability.

Obstructive Sleep Apnea

Known or suspected obstructive sleep apnea increases risk for perioperative airway obstruction, hypoxemia, opioid-related respiratory depression, and postoperative respiratory complications.

Known OSA Using PAP

Patient should bring PAP equipment when required by facility policy and use PAP whenever sleeping postoperatively. Respiratory therapy may assist with setup. Continuous pulse oximetry should be considered according to risk and institutional protocol.

OSA With PAP Intolerance or Nonadherence

For patients with known OSA who are unable or unwilling to use PAP, document the reason and duration of nonuse. Use continuous postoperative pulse oximetry when indicated, with supplemental oxygen and/or noninvasive positive-pressure ventilation if clinically warranted.

COPD

COPD severity should be determined from symptoms, exacerbation history, baseline oxygen requirement, pulmonary medications, functional capacity, prior hospitalizations, and pulmonary-function testing when available.

Optimization Prior to Surgery

  • Continue prescribed maintenance inhalers.
  • Ensure rescue bronchodilator availability.
  • Treat active exacerbation before elective surgery.
  • Evaluate new or worsening sputum, wheezing, or dyspnea.
  • Review baseline oxygen requirement.
  • Encourage smoking cessation.
  • Consider pulmonary consultation for severe or unstable disease.
Elective surgery should generally be postponed when the patient has an acute COPD exacerbation with clinically significant wheezing, worsening dyspnea, increased or purulent sputum, hypoxemia beyond baseline, or need for acute systemic treatment until adequately stabilized.

Institutional COPD / Respiratory Red Flags

Certain findings may indicate that an ambulatory or limited-acuity surgical setting is inappropriate and that a higher level of care should be considered.

Finding Concern
Continuous oxygen requirement Twenty-four-hour supplemental oxygen requirement may indicate advanced pulmonary disease.
FEV1 <50% predicted Suggests significant airflow limitation when interpreted in the appropriate clinical context.
BODE score ≥5 Indicates increased COPD disease burden and risk.
CAT score >10 Suggests clinically meaningful COPD symptom burden.
Arozullah score ≥4 Requires increased attention to postoperative respiratory risk under institutional criteria.
Chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure Higher perioperative respiratory risk and potential need for enhanced postoperative monitoring.
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis May require higher-level-of-care evaluation based on institutional criteria.

CAT Score

The COPD Assessment Test (CAT) provides a structured measure of COPD symptom burden.

Documentation format:

CAT score: ___

Increasing CAT scores reflect greater symptom burden and should be interpreted together with exacerbation history, oxygen use, spirometry, functional status, and current clinical stability.

BODE Index

The BODE index incorporates four major domains:

  • Body mass index
  • Airflow obstruction
  • Dyspnea
  • Exercise capacity
Documentation format:

BODE score: ___
Under institutional screening criteria, a BODE score of 5 or greater may warrant evaluation for a higher level of perioperative care.

Asthma

Patients with well-controlled asthma can generally proceed with elective surgery. Determine current symptom frequency, rescue-inhaler use, recent systemic steroid use, prior hospitalization or intubation, and current presence of wheezing.

Continue maintenance inhalers through the perioperative period unless specifically contraindicated.
New significant wheezing, acute exacerbation, respiratory distress, or uncontrolled symptoms should be treated before elective surgery.

Pulmonary Function Tests

Pulmonary function tests should not be obtained routinely before nonthoracic surgery. They are most useful when results will clarify the severity or cause of respiratory disease and are expected to influence management.

Common Spirometry Measurements

Measurement General Interpretation
FEV1 Forced expiratory volume during the first second of expiration. Reduction may reflect obstructive disease or reduced lung volume.
FVC Forced vital capacity. A reduced value may occur with restrictive disease, obstruction with air trapping, or suboptimal effort.
FEV1/FVC A reduced ratio supports airflow obstruction when below the appropriate lower limit of normal.
TLC Total lung capacity. A reduced TLC confirms restrictive physiology.
DLCO Measures gas-transfer capacity and may be reduced with emphysema, interstitial lung disease, pulmonary vascular disease, or anemia.

Basic PFT Interpretation

Pattern Typical Findings
Obstructive Reduced FEV1/FVC ratio. Common causes include COPD and asthma.
Restrictive Reduced lung volumes, particularly TLC, with preserved or increased FEV1/FVC ratio.
Mixed Evidence of both obstruction and reduced total lung capacity.
Reduced DLCO Consider emphysema, interstitial lung disease, pulmonary vascular disease, anemia, or other impaired gas-exchange states.
PFT abnormalities should not be interpreted in isolation. Preoperative decisions should incorporate symptoms, resting oxygenation, functional status, physical examination, prior exacerbations, and the planned procedure.

Chest X-Ray

Routine preoperative chest radiography is not recommended in asymptomatic patients solely because surgery is planned.

Consider imaging when clinically indicated by new respiratory symptoms, suspected pneumonia, unexplained hypoxemia, significant cardiopulmonary disease, abnormal examination findings, or another specific diagnostic question.

Recent Respiratory Infection

Determine the type and severity of infection, current symptoms, treatment, oxygenation, fever status, residual cough or wheezing, and whether the patient has returned to pulmonary baseline.

Active lower respiratory infection, pneumonia, significant wheezing, fever, new oxygen requirement, or unresolved respiratory compromise is a reason to reconsider proceeding with elective surgery until the patient improves.

A fixed waiting period is not appropriate for every infection. Surgical timing should be individualized based on illness severity, residual symptoms, comorbidities, surgery type, anesthesia plan, and facility criteria.

Smoking

Smoking increases pulmonary, cardiovascular, and wound complications. Encourage cessation as early as possible before surgery.

Longer abstinence generally provides greater benefit, but patients should still be encouraged to stop smoking even when surgery is approaching.

Obesity and Obesity Hypoventilation

Obesity alone does not necessarily preclude elective surgery, but severe obesity may increase airway difficulty, hypoventilation, OSA risk, thromboembolic risk, and opioid-related respiratory compromise.

Suspect obesity hypoventilation syndrome when severe obesity is associated with daytime hypercapnia, unexplained hypoxemia, severe OSA, or evidence of chronic hypoventilation.

Known or suspected chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure may require pulmonary evaluation and a higher-acuity perioperative setting.

Postoperative Pulmonary Optimization

Postoperative prevention strategies should be tailored to the patient's risk profile and procedure.

Strategy Purpose
Early mobilization Reduces atelectasis, venous stasis, and deconditioning.
Incentive spirometry Encourages lung expansion when incorporated into a broader respiratory care strategy.
EZPAP / lung expansion therapy May assist selected patients with postoperative lung expansion.
Bronchodilator therapy Treats bronchospasm and supports patients with reactive airway disease or COPD when clinically indicated.
PAP during sleep Reduces upper-airway obstruction in patients with OSA who use PAP.
Continuous pulse oximetry Detects postoperative hypoxemia in higher-risk patients.
Opioid-sparing analgesia Reduces medication-related respiratory depression when appropriate.

Common Postoperative Respiratory Orders

PAP anytime asleep.

Respiratory therapy to assist with PAP setup as needed.

Continuous pulse oximetry for patients at increased respiratory risk.

Supplemental oxygen and/or NIPPV if clinically warranted.

Incentive spirometry and aggressive pulmonary hygiene.

Xopenex / bronchodilator treatment as clinically indicated.

EZPAP as clinically indicated.

When Pulmonology Consultation Is Appropriate

Pulmonary consultation may be appropriate when the patient has severe or poorly controlled COPD, unexplained hypoxemia, chronic respiratory failure, significant restrictive lung disease, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, pulmonary hypertension, abnormal PFTs requiring clarification, recurrent respiratory exacerbations, or unresolved respiratory symptoms.

As with cardiology consultation, pulmonary referral should answer a specific clinical question rather than simply obtain a generic statement of “pulmonary clearance.”

Reasons to Consider Postponing Elective Surgery

Consider postponement when clinically significant respiratory instability is present, including:
  • Active pneumonia
  • Acute COPD exacerbation
  • Acute asthma exacerbation
  • Unexplained new hypoxemia
  • New or increasing oxygen requirement
  • Significant active wheezing
  • Respiratory distress
  • Unresolved lower respiratory infection
  • Acute hypercapnic respiratory failure

Preoperative Decision Framework

Question Action
Does the patient have active respiratory symptoms? Determine severity and evaluate for acute pulmonary disease.
Is chronic lung disease stable? Continue maintenance therapy and optimize reversible factors.
Is oxygenation at baseline? Investigate unexplained hypoxemia or increased oxygen requirement.
Is OSA known or suspected? Document STOP-BANG, PAP use, and postoperative monitoring plan.
Are PFTs needed? Order selectively when results will clarify disease severity or change management.
Does the patient meet higher-level-of-care criteria? Review institutional exclusions and discuss appropriate surgical venue.
Is pulmonary consultation needed? Refer when significant pulmonary risk remains unresolved.

Key Takeaways

  • Assess current respiratory symptoms and baseline oxygenation.
  • Do not routinely order PFTs or chest X-rays without an indication.
  • Use STOP-BANG for structured OSA screening.
  • Document PAP adherence or intolerance in known OSA.
  • Evaluate COPD severity using symptoms, exacerbations, oxygen requirement, and available PFT data.
  • Use CAT, BODE, and Arozullah tools when clinically appropriate.
  • Recognize institutional higher-level-of-care pulmonary criteria.
  • Treat active pulmonary exacerbations before elective surgery.
  • Plan postoperative PAP, oximetry, pulmonary hygiene, and bronchodilator therapy when indicated.
