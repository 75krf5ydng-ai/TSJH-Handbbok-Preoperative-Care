Purpose of This Reference Page

This page identifies the principal institutional guidance, professional-society guidelines, clinical practice standards, validated risk instruments and supporting references used throughout the TSJH Preoperative Optimization Handbook.

Important: TSJH institutional guidance and approved institutional amendments remain the primary source for facility-specific eligibility, escalation, medication-hold and higher-level-of-care pathways. National and society guidelines are included to support clinical interpretation and provide additional context. When recommendations differ, the more restrictive applicable facility, anesthesia, surgeon or specialty requirement should be followed when appropriate.

TSJH Institutional Guidance

Texas Spine & Joint Hospital Perioperative Consensus Guidelines.

Current TSJH institutional perioperative optimization guidance, including approved written amendments and annotations incorporated into the working institutional reference used for this handbook.

Facility-specific guidance includes, among other topics:
  • Cardiovascular eligibility and higher-level-of-care criteria
  • Heart-failure and ejection-fraction thresholds
  • Aortic and mitral valve disease
  • Pulmonary hypertension and right-heart findings
  • LBBB evaluation
  • BNP/NT-proBNP screening and postoperative cardiac surveillance
  • Pulmonary risk and higher-acuity criteria
  • Anticoagulant and antiplatelet quick-reference intervals
  • SGLT2 inhibitor interruption
  • AAA surveillance and interval comparison
  • Procedure- and venue-specific escalation criteria

Institutional guidance should be reviewed whenever TSJH policy is revised. The handbook should not be interpreted as replacing current hospital policy, anesthesia judgment, surgeon requirements or specialty consultation.

Anticoagulation, Antiplatelet & Periprocedural Medication Management

Thompson, A., Fleischmann, K. E., Smilowitz, N. R., et al. (2024). 2024 AHA/ACC/ACS/ASNC/HRS/SCA/SCCT/SCMR/SVM guideline for perioperative cardiovascular management for noncardiac surgery. Circulation, 150, e351–e442. https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001285

The handbook distinguishes TSJH medication-hold quick references from broader guideline-based interruption intervals. Anticoagulant and antiplatelet management should account for procedural bleeding risk, renal function, neuraxial or deep plexus requirements, recent thrombosis, mechanical valve status, recent PCI/stent, DAPT requirements and the indication for treatment.

Recent PCI/stent or required dual antiplatelet therapy should not be managed by a routine medication-hold table alone. Multidisciplinary review with cardiology, surgery and anesthesia is appropriate before elective interruption when clinically required.

Diabetes, SGLT2 Inhibitors & Perioperative Glycemic Management

American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee. (2026). Diabetes care in the hospital: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026. Diabetes Care, 49(Supplement 1), S339–S361.

The 2026 ADA Standards support a preoperative A1C goal below 8% when achievable within approximately 3 months of elective surgery and a perioperative glucose target generally between 100 and 180 mg/dL. A1C alone should not be used as the sole reason to postpone surgery.

Current perioperative guidance supports withholding SGLT2 inhibitors before scheduled surgery because of the risk of euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis. The handbook identifies the 3-day interval for most SGLT2 inhibitors and 4-day interval for ertugliflozin while preserving applicable TSJH and anesthesia requirements.

GLP-1 / GIP Receptor Agonists

Perioperative management of GLP-1 and dual GIP/GLP-1 therapies should be individualized according to aspiration risk rather than based solely on medication class. The handbook incorporates assessment of dose-escalation phase, significant gastrointestinal symptoms, higher dosing, gastroparesis or delayed gastric emptying risk, procedure characteristics and anesthesia recommendations.

Patients without significant gastrointestinal symptoms or other delayed-gastric- emptying risk factors may be managed differently from patients with clinically important aspiration-risk features.

Pulmonary, OSA & Respiratory Risk

Pulmonary risk assessment in this handbook incorporates the patient's clinical status, oxygen requirement, COPD symptom burden, pulmonary-function findings, sleep-disordered breathing, PAP use, pulmonary hypertension, right-heart disease, frailty and anticipated postoperative respiratory requirements.

TSJH-specific higher-risk pulmonary thresholds and venue criteria are identified as institutional guidance rather than universal national cutoffs.

Validated tools incorporated or referenced within the handbook include STOP-BANG, the Arozullah Respiratory Failure Risk Index, CAT and BODE where clinically appropriate.

Infection, Urinary Screening & Asymptomatic Bacteriuria

Nicolle, L. E., Gupta, K., Bradley, S. F., et al. (2019). Clinical practice guideline for the management of asymptomatic bacteriuria: 2019 update by the Infectious Diseases Society of America. Clinical Infectious Diseases, 68(10), e83–e110. https://doi.org/10.1093/cid/ciy1121

The IDSA guideline recommends against routine screening for or treatment of asymptomatic bacteriuria in patients undergoing elective nonurologic surgery. Symptomatic urinary infection and urologic procedures involving mucosal trauma require separate assessment.

The handbook therefore distinguishes abnormal urinalysis findings from true symptomatic urinary tract infection and discourages unnecessary antimicrobial treatment of incidental bacteriuria in otherwise asymptomatic nonurologic surgical patients.

Renal, Electrolyte & Metabolic Assessment

Renal guidance in this handbook emphasizes comparison with the patient's baseline, differentiation of stable chronic kidney disease from acute kidney injury, assessment of volume status, management of clinically significant sodium, potassium and bicarbonate abnormalities, coordination of dialysis, and review of nephrotoxic or renally cleared medications.

Numeric renal and electrolyte alerts within interactive tools are clinical decision-support thresholds and should be interpreted in the context of symptoms, chronicity, renal function, medications, anesthesia requirements and the planned procedure.

Anemia & Hematologic Optimization

Preoperative anemia evaluation should identify the likely etiology rather than relying on hemoglobin alone. The handbook incorporates CBC indices, iron studies, transferrin saturation, ferritin and other appropriate testing to distinguish common causes of anemia and guide appropriate optimization.

Anticoagulation and hematologic abnormalities should be evaluated in the context of procedural bleeding risk, neuraxial requirements and the patient's underlying thrombotic risk.

Functional Capacity & Validated Risk Instruments

Duke Activity Status Index (DASI)

Hlatky, M. A., Boineau, R. E., Higginbotham, M. B., Lee, K. L., Mark, D. B., Califf, R. M., Cobb, F. R., & Pryor, D. B. (1989). A brief self-administered questionnaire to determine functional capacity (the Duke Activity Status Index). American Journal of Cardiology, 64(10), 651–654. https://doi.org/10.1016/0002-9149(89)90496-7

The handbook uses self-reported DASI as the primary functional-capacity screen rather than relying solely on subjective MET estimates.

Revised Cardiac Risk Index (RCRI)

The Revised Cardiac Risk Index is incorporated as part of perioperative cardiac risk estimation and the TSJH/TSJR biomarker-screening pathway.

STOP-BANG

STOP-BANG is used as a standardized screening instrument for obstructive sleep apnea risk and should be interpreted with known OSA diagnosis, PAP use, symptoms and postoperative monitoring requirements.

Clinical Frailty Scale

The Clinical Frailty Scale is used to support assessment of physiologic reserve, mobility, postoperative support needs and disposition planning.

Surveillance Imaging & Specialty Follow-Up

The handbook emphasizes disease-specific surveillance rather than automatic repeat testing simply because surgery is scheduled.

Repeat imaging or specialty review is prompted by factors such as new symptoms, interval progression, overdue surveillance, unresolved prior abnormalities or results that could meaningfully alter perioperative management.

TSJH-specific AAA guidance includes comparison of serial surveillance studies for aneurysms greater than 4.0 cm, typically approximately 1 year apart, to establish stability and absence of interval growth when applicable.

Use of External Clinical Resources

External clinical decision-support resources referenced by the handbook may include MDCalc, OpenEvidence and professional-society guideline resources. These resources supplement rather than replace clinical judgment or current institutional policy.

Because clinical guidelines are periodically revised, users should verify time-sensitive medication, anticoagulation, diabetes, cardiac and anesthesia recommendations against the most current applicable guidance.

Reference Hierarchy Used by This Handbook

  1. Current TSJH institutional policy and approved institutional amendments
  2. Current anesthesia, surgeon and specialty-specific requirements
  3. Current national or multisociety clinical practice guidelines
  4. Validated clinical risk instruments and peer-reviewed literature
  5. Clinical judgment based on the individual patient's condition and planned procedure
The designation Optimized indicates that the patient meets the applicable perioperative optimization pathway at the time of assessment. Pending indicates that a clinically meaningful issue remains unresolved. Not optimized indicates an active or unstable issue requiring treatment, evaluation or an alternative perioperative plan before routine elective surgery.

Clinical Disclaimer

This handbook is intended as clinical decision support for trained health care professionals. It does not replace physician or anesthesia judgment, specialty consultation, current hospital policy, manufacturer recommendations or individualized patient assessment.

Guidelines, medication recommendations and institutional policies may change. The most current applicable source should be reviewed when a recommendation could materially affect perioperative safety.
