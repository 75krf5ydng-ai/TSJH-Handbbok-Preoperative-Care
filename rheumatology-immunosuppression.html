Rheumatology & Immunosuppression

Perioperative assessment of inflammatory arthritis, autoimmune disease, DMARDs, biologic therapy, JAK inhibitors, corticosteroids and infection risk.

← Return to Handbook

Purpose

This page supports perioperative medication review and medical optimization for patients with inflammatory arthritis, autoimmune disease or immunosuppressive therapy undergoing elective noncardiac surgery.

Balance two competing risks: postoperative infection and impaired wound healing versus disease flare caused by interruption of therapy.

Medication decisions should be individualized with the prescribing rheumatologist when disease is complex, therapy is unfamiliar, or surgery carries substantial infection consequences.

Preoperative Rheumatology Screening

  • Identify the autoimmune or inflammatory diagnosis.
  • Determine whether disease is stable, active or flaring.
  • List all conventional DMARDs, biologics, JAK inhibitors and corticosteroids.
  • Confirm the medication dosing interval and date of the last dose.
  • Assess recent infections, antibiotic treatment or hospitalization.
  • Review CBC, renal function and liver function when clinically indicated.
  • Assess chronic steroid exposure and risk of adrenal suppression.
  • Identify associated organ involvement such as lung, cardiac, renal or hematologic disease.
  • Determine whether rheumatology input is needed before medication interruption.

Conventional DMARDs

For many patients undergoing elective total hip or total knee arthroplasty, commonly used conventional DMARDs may be continued through surgery.
Medication Typical Perioperative Approach Important Considerations
Methotrexate Generally continue through surgery when disease is stable and no major contraindication exists. Review renal function, hepatic function, cytopenias and active infection.
Hydroxychloroquine Generally continue. Usually does not require perioperative interruption.
Sulfasalazine Generally continue. Review CBC and hepatic function when clinically appropriate.
Leflunomide Generally continue for elective THA/TKA under commonly used rheumatology guidance. Consider hepatic function, cytopenias, infection history and specialist recommendations.

Biologic Therapy

For elective hip and knee arthroplasty, biologic agents are commonly withheld through surgery and the operation is scheduled after the next dose would have been due.

The practical rule is generally to hold the biologic for approximately one dosing interval rather than using the medication half-life alone.

Medication Typical Dosing Common Surgical Timing Concept
Adalimumab Usually every 2 weeks Schedule surgery after the next scheduled dose would have been due.
Etanercept Usually weekly Hold through surgery; schedule after one dosing interval.
Infliximab Commonly every 4, 6 or 8 weeks Schedule surgery near the end of the dosing cycle after the next dose is withheld.
Certolizumab Commonly every 2 or 4 weeks Hold for one dosing interval.
Golimumab Monthly or IV every 8 weeks Schedule after the next dose would have been due.
Abatacept Weekly SQ or monthly IV Hold for one dosing interval before surgery.
Tocilizumab Weekly SQ or every 4 weeks IV Hold for one dosing interval.
Sarilumab Usually every 2 weeks Hold for one dosing interval.
Secukinumab Usually every 4 weeks after loading Schedule surgery after the next dose would have been due.
Ixekizumab Usually every 4 weeks after loading Hold for one dosing interval.
Ustekinumab Often every 12 weeks Schedule surgery after the next dose would have been due.
Risankizumab Long interval dosing Coordinate exact timing with prescribing specialist.
Belimumab Weekly SQ or monthly IV Decision depends on disease severity and dosing regimen; specialist coordination may be needed.

JAK Inhibitors

JAK inhibitors generally require temporary interruption before elective surgery. Current rheumatology guidance commonly uses a shorter preoperative interruption than older recommendations.
Medication Typical Approach
Tofacitinib Withhold before surgery according to current rheumatology guidance; commonly surgery is planned after approximately a 3-day interruption.
Baricitinib Commonly withhold for approximately 3 days before surgery.
Upadacitinib Commonly withhold for approximately 3 days before surgery.
JAK inhibitors may also carry thrombotic and infection-related considerations. Review the patient's individual VTE risk, infection history and indication for therapy.

Restarting Held Antirheumatic Therapy

A commonly used postoperative approach is to restart held antirheumatic therapy once the surgical wound demonstrates satisfactory healing and there is no evidence of active infection.

For arthroplasty patients, this is often approximately 14 days after surgery, but timing should be individualized.

Before restarting, assess for:

  • Wound healing progression
  • Drainage
  • Significant erythema
  • Swelling concerning for infection
  • Systemic infection
  • Ongoing antibiotic treatment
  • Surgeon concern regarding the incision

Corticosteroids

Chronic corticosteroid exposure increases infection risk, hyperglycemia, poor wound healing, osteoporosis and risk of hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal suppression.

Preoperative Review

  • Document steroid name and daily dose.
  • Determine duration of treatment.
  • Determine whether steroids were recently tapered or discontinued.
  • Assess for adrenal suppression risk.
  • Avoid unnecessary abrupt interruption of chronic glucocorticoid therapy.
  • Consider perioperative glucose monitoring.
Patients receiving chronic glucocorticoids generally continue their usual daily dose. Additional perioperative steroid supplementation should be individualized according to adrenal suppression risk, surgical stress and anesthesia/medical protocol.

Systemic Lupus Erythematosus

SLE requires additional attention because both the underlying disease and immunosuppressive treatment may alter perioperative risk.

Assess for:

  • Renal involvement or lupus nephritis
  • Cardiac disease or pericarditis
  • Pulmonary involvement
  • Cytopenias
  • Antiphospholipid syndrome
  • Prior thrombosis
  • Active infection
  • Current disease activity
  • Glucocorticoid exposure
  • Immunosuppressive medication regimen
Medication interruption in severe SLE requires particular caution because withdrawal of therapy may precipitate a clinically important flare. Coordinate complex immunosuppressive regimens with rheumatology.

Common SLE Immunosuppressants

Medication Perioperative Consideration
Mycophenolate mofetil Management depends on disease severity. Patients with severe organ-threatening SLE may be managed differently from those with non-severe disease.
Azathioprine Continuation versus temporary interruption depends on disease severity and specialist guidance.
Cyclosporine Review renal function, blood pressure, drug interactions and indication.
Tacrolimus Coordinate with specialist; monitor renal function and medication interactions.

Rituximab Long biologic effect requires deliberate surgical timing and rheumatology coordination.

Rituximab

Do not apply a simple short medication hold to rituximab. Its biologic effect is prolonged, and elective surgery should be coordinated with the treatment cycle and prescribing specialist.

Document the indication, last infusion date, next planned infusion, recent infections, immunoglobulin concerns if known and disease stability.

Rheumatoid Arthritis

Rheumatoid arthritis may create perioperative considerations beyond medication management.

Consider:

  • Cervical spine disease or instability when clinically suspected
  • Difficult airway risk
  • Temporomandibular joint limitation
  • Pulmonary disease
  • Cardiovascular disease
  • Anemia
  • Chronic steroid exposure
  • Baseline functional limitation
Patients with neurologic symptoms, significant neck pain, known cervical instability or advanced rheumatoid cervical disease may require additional evaluation before airway manipulation.

Inflammatory Myopathy / Systemic Sclerosis

Systemic Sclerosis
  • Evaluate pulmonary hypertension risk.
  • Review interstitial lung disease.
  • Assess renal involvement.
  • Consider severe reflux and aspiration risk.
  • Review difficult airway or limited mouth opening.
Dermatomyositis / Polymyositis
  • Assess respiratory muscle involvement.
  • Review swallowing dysfunction and aspiration risk.
  • Assess cardiac involvement when clinically indicated.
  • Document baseline weakness.

Infection Screening

Elective surgery should generally be deferred when a clinically important active infection is present until the infection has been adequately treated and the patient is clinically appropriate to proceed.

Ask about:

  • Recent fever or chills
  • Respiratory infection
  • Urinary symptoms
  • Skin or soft-tissue infection
  • Dental infection when clinically relevant
  • Recent antibiotic therapy
  • Recent hospitalization
  • Recurrent opportunistic infection

Laboratory Review

Testing should be individualized to the medication and disease.

Test Why It May Matter
CBC Anemia, leukopenia, neutropenia or thrombocytopenia related to disease or therapy.
Creatinine / eGFR Methotrexate clearance, lupus nephritis, calcineurin inhibitor toxicity and medication dosing.
AST / ALT Methotrexate, leflunomide and other medication-associated hepatotoxicity.
Glucose Chronic steroid treatment and perioperative hyperglycemia.

When to Contact Rheumatology

  • Uncertain biologic or immunosuppressant hold interval
  • Severe or organ-threatening autoimmune disease
  • Recent disease flare
  • Recent initiation or escalation of therapy
  • Rituximab treatment
  • Complex SLE medication regimen
  • Significant cytopenia
  • Recurrent or opportunistic infection
  • Concern that medication interruption may cause serious disease deterioration
  • Unclear timing for postoperative medication resumption

Key Perioperative Principles

  • Do not automatically stop every immunosuppressive medication.
  • Conventional DMARDs and biologic agents are managed differently.
  • Biologic timing is generally based on the dosing interval.
  • JAK inhibitors typically require a short preoperative interruption.
  • Restart held therapy only after satisfactory wound healing and absence of infection.
  • Do not abruptly discontinue chronic corticosteroids without a clinical plan.
  • Severe SLE requires individualized medication management.
  • Rituximab requires deliberate specialist coordination.
  • Active infection takes priority over elective surgical scheduling.

Clinical Reference Note

Perioperative antirheumatic medication management should follow current specialty guidance, institutional policy and the prescribing clinician's recommendations. Recommendations may differ by surgery type, autoimmune disease severity, infection risk and medication indication.
