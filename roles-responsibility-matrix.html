Preoperative Team Roles, Scope & Responsibility Matrix

Who collects, interprets, decides, escalates and closes each preoperative issue

Home RN Workflow Provider Workflow Anesthesia Communication Resources

Purpose

Preoperative care is most efficient when each team member knows which tasks they own, which tasks require collaboration and which decisions belong to another discipline.

This matrix is designed to reduce duplicated work, missed follow-up and vague handoffs.
Actual scope is governed by professional licensure, facility policy, approved standing orders, credentialing and medical staff bylaws. This guide does not expand scope beyond those requirements.

Core Principle

The person who identifies a clinically significant unresolved issue is responsible for making sure it is routed to the appropriate owner. The person responsible for the final decision should clearly document that the issue has been closed.

Perform medication reconciliation
Identify high-risk medications
Collect vitals
Complete approved screening tools
Obtain records
Summarize objective specialist testing
Initiate formally approved standing orders

RN Record Summarization

Nursing staff can make the provider's review substantially faster by organizing records in a consistent way.

Date: ___
Visit type: ___
Specialist: ___
Diagnosis: ___
Objective testing: ___
Interpretation from report: ___
Recommendations documented by specialist: ___
Summarize what the report says rather than independently making a new medical interpretation.

RN — Appropriate Statements

“Echo dated 06/12/26 reports EF 55–60% with mild MR.”

“Cardiology note dated 07/01/26 states CAD is stable without angina.”

“Patient reports last Eliquis dose was 08/18/26 at 8 PM.”

“EKG today differs from prior tracing; provider notified.”

“Current UA is abnormal, but patient denies urinary symptoms.”

RN — Avoid Independent Conclusions

Avoid:

“Patient is cleared.”
“EKG is not concerning.”
“Cardiology is not needed.”
“Patient needs a stress test.”
“Patient needs bridging.”
“Urine culture requires antibiotics.”
“Patient is optimized.”
“Patient is safe for outpatient surgery.”

Conditions the Preoperative Provider Can Often Manage Directly

Condition Potential Direct Management
Stable hypertension Review adherence, trend and perioperative medication plan.
Mild anemia Classify, order focused workup and initiate treatment when appropriate.
Iron deficiency Initiate oral/IV pathway as appropriate and assess source.
Stable CKD Review renal trend, electrolytes, medications and nephrotoxins.
Stable COPD/asthma Confirm current inhalers and absence of exacerbation.
OSA Assess PAP use/intolerance and postoperative respiratory plan.
Stable diabetes Review glycemic control and establish perioperative medication plan.
Abnormal UA without symptoms Interpret clinically rather than automatically treating.
Mild stable lab abnormalities Compare prior values and decide whether repeat testing is meaningful.

PCP Responsibilities

The PCP is most useful when a condition requires longitudinal management rather than a one-time perioperative decision.

Examples

  • New chronic diabetes management
  • Long-term hypertension titration
  • New chronic disease diagnosis
  • Long-term medication adjustment
  • Nonurgent outpatient diagnostic workup

Do Not Use PCP as a Default “Clearance” Service

If the preoperative provider can reasonably interpret and optimize the finding, do not automatically delay surgery simply to obtain a PCP signature.

Who Closes the Pending Item?

Issue identified
↓
Owner assigned
↓
Required action completed
↓
Result reviewed by responsible clinician
↓
Decision documented
↓
Final optimization status updated
A result being uploaded into the chart does not mean the pending item is closed. Someone must review it and document its impact on the surgical plan.

When Two Services Disagree

Different disciplines may appropriately emphasize different risks. The goal is to identify the specific point of disagreement and resolve it through direct clinician-to-clinician communication.

Examples

  • Cardiology recommends aspirin continuation while surgeon requests hold
  • Surgeon considers venue acceptable while anesthesia requests HLOC
  • Provider considers testing adequate while consultant recommends more testing
  • Anticoagulation restart timing differs between thrombotic and bleeding priorities

Key Takeaways

  • RN staff collect, verify, summarize and escalate.
  • The preoperative provider interprets, risk stratifies and assigns medical optimization status.
  • Anesthesia owns airway, anesthetic, aspiration, neuraxial and intraoperative management decisions.
  • The surgeon owns procedure-specific bleeding tolerance, operative decisions and DVT prophylaxis.
  • Specialists should answer focused questions rather than provide generic clearance.
  • PCP referral is not required for every abnormality.
  • Standing orders must be formally approved.
  • A pending item is not closed merely because a result appears in the chart.
  • Conflicting recommendations should be resolved through direct communication.
  • The final goal is one coherent perioperative plan with clear ownership.
