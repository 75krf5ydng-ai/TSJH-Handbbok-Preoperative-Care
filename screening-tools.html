DASI, RCRI, STOP-BANG, CFS, AWOL-S, Arozullah, CAT and BODE

Home Evaluation Cardiac Pulmonary Communication

Purpose

Structured screening tools improve consistency in preoperative evaluation and help identify patients who may require additional testing, postoperative monitoring or a higher level of care.

Screening tools support clinical judgment; they do not replace it. Scores should be interpreted together with the patient's symptoms, comorbidities, planned procedure and institutional criteria.

Duke Activity Status Index — DASI

DASI is a patient-reported assessment of functional capacity based on the ability to perform common daily activities.

DASI is preferred over an informal estimate of METs because it provides a structured and reproducible measure of functional capacity.

Clinical Use

  • Estimate functional capacity before noncardiac surgery
  • Assist with cardiovascular risk assessment
  • Identify patients with poor or uncertain functional status
  • Support decisions about whether further cardiac testing is useful
Documentation:

DASI: ___
Higher DASI scores generally indicate better functional capacity.

RCRI and BNP Screening

Under the TSJR/TSJH pathway, BNP or NT-proBNP screening is obtained for selected patients based on age and RCRI/cardiovascular risk criteria.
Marker Threshold Postoperative Action
BNP ≥92 pg/mL Postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.
NT-proBNP ≥300 ng/L Postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.
BNP screening. Obtain BNP because of age greater than 65 years and RCRI score of 1. If BNP is 92 pg/mL or greater, implement the TSJH postoperative cardiac-surveillance protocol with postoperative EKG and daily troponins for 48–72 hours.

AWOL-S Delirium Risk Screening

AWOL-S is used to estimate risk for postoperative delirium and may help identify patients who need delirium-prevention strategies.

Use When

  • Older surgical patient
  • Known cognitive impairment
  • History of delirium
  • Frailty
  • Multiple delirium risk factors
Documentation:

AWOL-S: ___
A higher delirium-risk score should prompt proactive prevention, including medication review, orientation, sleep protection, mobility, hydration and sensory support.

Arozullah Respiratory Failure Risk Index

The Arozullah index estimates risk of postoperative respiratory failure using patient and procedure characteristics.

Documentation:

Arozullah: ___
Under TSJH pulmonary criteria, Arozullah score ≥4 should prompt increased attention to postoperative respiratory risk and review of surgical-setting suitability.

COPD Assessment Test — CAT

CAT measures symptom burden in patients with COPD.

Documentation:

CAT: ___
Under TSJH pulmonary criteria, CAT score greater than 10 is an important pulmonary risk marker and should prompt further review.

CAT Helps Assess

  • Cough
  • Sputum
  • Chest tightness
  • Dyspnea
  • Activity limitation
  • Confidence leaving home
  • Sleep
  • Energy

BODE Index

BODE integrates multiple dimensions of COPD severity.

BODE

B = Body mass index
O = Airflow obstruction
D = Dyspnea
E = Exercise capacity
Documentation:

BODE: ___
Under TSJH pulmonary criteria, BODE score ≥5 represents significant pulmonary risk and may require higher-level-of-care review.

DASI Functional capacity Most adult preoperative patients when functional capacity matters.

AWOL-S Delirium risk Older or cognitively vulnerable surgical patients.

External Tool Use

External calculators should support, not replace, clinical judgment. Confirm that the calculator population and intended use match the patient being evaluated.

Record the resulting score in the preoperative note rather than relying solely on a screenshot or external webpage.

Screening Workflow

Step Action
1 Determine which screening tools apply to the patient.
2 Calculate the score using patient-reported and objective data.
3 Document the numeric result.
4 Apply TSJH/TSJR thresholds when relevant.
5 Identify whether additional testing or monitoring is required.
6 Incorporate the result into the final optimization decision.

Common Errors

Avoid:
  • Estimating a score without completing the actual tool
  • Using RCRI as the only measure of perioperative cardiac risk
  • Using STOP-BANG as a diagnosis of OSA
  • Using age alone as a substitute for frailty assessment
  • Ignoring a high pulmonary risk score because oxygen saturation is normal at rest
  • Documenting a score without acting on a clinically important threshold

Key Takeaways

  • Use DASI for structured functional-capacity assessment.
  • Use RCRI to support cardiac risk stratification.
  • Apply BNP/NT-proBNP screening when institutional criteria are met.
  • Use STOP-BANG for OSA screening.
  • Use CFS to identify frailty.
  • Use AWOL-S for delirium-risk assessment when appropriate.
  • Use Arozullah for postoperative respiratory-failure risk.
  • Use CAT and BODE in patients with COPD when clinically relevant.
  • Document numeric scores clearly and avoid unnecessary explanatory repetition.
  • Use MDCalc links as convenient tools, not as substitutes for clinical judgment.
