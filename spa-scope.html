SPA Provider & RN Scope, Optimization Authority & Escalation

Practical division of responsibilities in the preoperative assessment workflow

Purpose

Efficient preoperative care depends on a clear understanding of what can be evaluated and optimized within the SPA workflow, what can be performed under nursing standing orders, and what requires escalation to anesthesia, the surgeon, primary care or a specialist.

The goal is to resolve routine optimization issues before the day of surgery while avoiding unnecessary referrals for conditions that can be safely assessed and managed within the preoperative clinic.

Important Scope Principle

Scope of practice is determined by state law, licensure, credentials, privileges, collaborative or supervisory requirements when applicable, facility policy, and approved standing orders. This chapter describes a practical workflow framework and does not override those requirements.

Role of the SPA Provider

The SPA provider performs medical evaluation, risk stratification, optimization and perioperative planning for elective noncardiac surgery.

Core Responsibilities

  • Review and interpret the medical history
  • Perform focused history and physical examination
  • Assess current symptoms and disease stability
  • Review medications and perioperative medication issues
  • Interpret relevant preoperative laboratory results
  • Interpret EKG findings within scope and competency
  • Review prior cardiac, pulmonary and specialty testing
  • Calculate and document risk scores
  • Identify conditions that require additional evaluation
  • Initiate routine optimization within scope
  • Determine whether the patient is Optimized, Pending or Not optimized

What the SPA Provider Can Commonly Optimize Directly

Issue Possible SPA Action
Mild dehydration Address oral hydration when appropriate, review contributing medications and repeat BMP when indicated.
Mild stable electrolyte abnormality Review cause, medication contribution and need for repeat testing or replacement within facility scope.
Stable hypertension Review medication adherence, repeat blood pressure and determine whether severe or symptomatic hypertension requires escalation.
Stable COPD or asthma Confirm inhaler adherence, continue maintenance therapy and establish postoperative pulmonary plan.
OSA Document PAP use or intolerance and establish monitoring plan.
Mild anemia Classify anemia, order targeted studies when appropriate and initiate iron replacement or referral according to severity and cause.
Abnormal UA without symptoms Determine whether findings represent asymptomatic bacteriuria and avoid unnecessary treatment.
Medication reconciliation Clarify routine perioperative medication instructions according to established protocols.
Constipation risk Establish a perioperative bowel regimen when appropriate.

Issues That Often Require PCP Coordination

Primary-care involvement is useful when a chronic condition requires longitudinal management beyond the immediate perioperative period.

Examples

  • Newly identified chronic hypertension requiring long-term treatment
  • Previously undiagnosed diabetes
  • Persistent unexplained anemia requiring outpatient evaluation
  • New thyroid abnormality without acute instability
  • Persistent microscopic hematuria
  • Chronic renal dysfunction requiring longitudinal follow-up
  • Medication adherence concerns requiring ongoing management
PCP referral does not automatically mean elective surgery must be postponed. Determine whether the abnormality requires resolution before surgery or can safely be followed longitudinally.

When Cardiology Is Needed

Consider cardiology evaluation when there is:
  • Active or suspected myocardial ischemia
  • Decompensated heart failure
  • New significant arrhythmia
  • High-grade conduction disease
  • New or clinically important LBBB
  • Moderate-to-severe symptomatic valvular disease
  • Severe or inadequately characterized structural heart disease
  • Recent PCI or complex antiplatelet issue
  • Uncertain cardiovascular optimization

When Electrophysiology Is Needed

EP consultation may be appropriate for:
  • Pacemaker or ICD issue not adequately characterized
  • Device malfunction concern
  • Recent cardiac ablation with anticoagulation question
  • Complex arrhythmia management
  • Perioperative device programming question
  • Unclear pacing dependence

When Pulmonology Is Needed

Consider pulmonary evaluation for:
  • Unexplained significant dyspnea
  • FEV1 below facility threshold
  • New or increasing oxygen requirement
  • Chronic hypercapnia
  • Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis
  • Significant pulmonary hypertension
  • Frequent COPD exacerbations
  • Poorly controlled severe asthma
  • Unclear pulmonary diagnosis

When Nephrology Is Needed

Consider nephrology for:
  • Progressive AKI
  • Advanced or unstable CKD
  • Dialysis planning
  • Refractory hyperkalemia
  • Severe acid-base disorder
  • Complex volume-management issues
  • Unexplained rapid renal deterioration

When Hematology Is Needed

Consider hematology for:
  • Severe or unexplained anemia
  • Pancytopenia
  • Severe thrombocytopenia
  • Known bleeding disorder
  • Unexplained coagulopathy
  • Complex thrombophilia
  • Recurrent thrombosis
  • Complex bridging or anticoagulation problem

When Neurology Is Needed

Consider neurology for:
  • Recent stroke or TIA with unresolved timing questions
  • New focal neurologic symptoms
  • Uncontrolled seizures
  • Progressive neuromuscular weakness
  • Rapid cognitive decline
  • Complex neurologic medication management

When Endocrinology Is Needed

Consider endocrinology for:
  • Severely uncontrolled diabetes
  • Recurrent severe hypoglycemia
  • Complex insulin pump management
  • Recent DKA or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state
  • Adrenal insufficiency
  • Complex chronic steroid management
  • Uncontrolled thyrotoxicosis
  • Severe untreated hypothyroidism

Role of the Preoperative RN

The preoperative RN is essential to efficient data collection, medication reconciliation, record retrieval, protocol-driven screening and escalation of abnormal findings.

Common RN Responsibilities

  • Obtain and verify vital signs
  • Complete structured nursing history
  • Perform medication reconciliation
  • Verify allergies
  • Collect required screening information
  • Obtain records from PCP and specialists
  • Identify missing testing or documentation
  • Perform standing-order tasks when authorized
  • Escalate abnormal findings to the provider
  • Provide approved patient instructions

RN Medical Record Summarization

Nursing staff can play a major role in organizing outside records before the provider review.

RN summarization may include extracting dates of visits and testing, identifying diagnoses, listing medication recommendations and organizing records chronologically. Final medical interpretation and perioperative decision-making remain the responsibility of the appropriately credentialed provider.

Standing Orders

Standing orders can reduce delays when they clearly define which tests or actions nursing staff may initiate under specified conditions.

Standing orders must be formally approved by the facility and medical leadership. Nursing staff should not independently create diagnostic or treatment plans outside those protocols.

Potential Standing-Order Categories

  • CBC based on procedure or blood-loss criteria
  • BMP/CMP based on age, medications or renal disease
  • A1c based on diabetes protocol
  • EKG based on age/cardiovascular criteria
  • BNP/NT-proBNP based on approved RCRI/age pathway
  • MRSA or infection screening when required
  • Pregnancy testing when applicable
  • Urine testing only when facility or procedure criteria are met

RN Red Flags for Immediate Provider Review

Escalate promptly for:
  • Chest pain
  • New or worsening dyspnea
  • Syncope or near-syncope
  • New focal neurologic symptoms
  • Markedly abnormal vital signs
  • New oxygen requirement
  • Active wheezing or respiratory distress
  • Recent hospitalization not previously reviewed
  • Recent PCI, stroke, PE, DVT or major cardiac event
  • Unclear anticoagulation plan
  • Critical laboratory result
  • New fever or suspected infection

Medication Issues the RN Can Identify but Should Escalate

Medication Issue RN Action

Clopidogrel or other P2Y12 inhibitor Identify indication and possible recent PCI; escalate before instructing interruption unless approved protocol applies.
SGLT2 inhibitor Identify agent and verify approved preoperative hold instructions.
GLP-1 receptor agonist Identify agent, dosing schedule and GI symptoms; escalate according to anesthesia protocol.
Insulin pump Identify device and regimen; ensure provider/anesthesia plan exists.
Chronic steroid therapy Identify medication, dose and duration; escalate for adrenal-risk review.

When Anesthesia Should Be Contacted

Some concerns are best resolved by anesthesia because they relate directly to airway management, aspiration, neuraxial safety or anesthetic technique.

Examples

  • Complex airway history
  • Prior difficult intubation
  • Severe OSA with PAP intolerance
  • Potential aspiration risk
  • GLP-1 therapy with significant GI symptoms
  • Neuraxial anticoagulation concern
  • Severe pulmonary disease near facility exclusion threshold
  • Recent stroke timing question under anesthesia policy
  • Unusual anesthetic reaction or malignant hyperthermia history

When the Surgeon Should Be Contacted

The surgeon should be included when the issue directly affects surgical timing, bleeding risk, hardware implantation, postoperative medication resumption or whether the expected benefit justifies increased risk.

Examples

  • Need to delay surgery for medical optimization
  • Antiplatelet resumption after spine or joint surgery
  • Significant anemia before high-blood-loss procedure
  • Active infection near surgery date
  • Procedure-specific aspirin hold question
  • Recent stroke or cardiac event affecting timing
  • Need for higher-acuity venue

Optimization vs “Clearance”

The SPA provider's role is to assess risk, identify modifiable conditions, optimize those conditions when possible and document residual perioperative risk. Avoid reducing this process to a binary “clearance” statement.

A patient can be medically optimized while still carrying chronic residual risk.

Final Status Categories

Status Meaning
Optimized No unresolved medical issue currently prevents proceeding under the planned perioperative pathway.
Pending Additional information, testing, treatment or specialist input is required before final determination.
Not optimized A clinically significant unresolved condition currently makes proceeding inappropriate or outside facility criteria.

Examples of “Pending”

  • Awaiting BNP result
  • Awaiting repeat potassium
  • Awaiting cardiology review of new LBBB
  • Awaiting anticoagulation hold instructions
  • Awaiting pulmonary assessment
  • Awaiting repeat CBC after anemia treatment
  • Awaiting device interrogation

Examples of “Not Optimized”

Examples include:
  • Active acute coronary syndrome
  • Decompensated heart failure
  • Severe unresolved hyperkalemia
  • Active pneumonia
  • Acute COPD exacerbation
  • DKA or hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state
  • Acute stroke/TIA symptoms
  • Severe uncontrolled infection
  • Unresolved major anticoagulation issue for neuraxial surgery

Same-Day Cancellation Prevention

Many same-day cancellations occur because an abnormality was identified but not fully resolved before the procedure date.

High-Yield Prevention Strategies

  • Identify anticoagulants early
  • Verify specialist records before finalizing the H&P
  • Resolve abnormal potassium and renal function before surgery day
  • Confirm SGLT2 and GLP-1 instructions
  • Address active respiratory symptoms early
  • Clarify recent cardiac/stroke events
  • Obtain device information before surgery
  • Document all pending items with an owner and deadline

Pending-Item Ownership

Communication Escalation Pathway

Level Action
1 RN identifies missing information or abnormal finding and routes to SPA provider.
2 SPA provider determines whether issue can be resolved internally.
3 If not, contact anesthesia, surgeon, PCP or appropriate specialist.
4 Document recommendation, responsibility and expected completion date.
5 Verify completion before final optimization status is assigned.

Decision Framework

Question Action
Can the abnormality be safely optimized within SPA scope? Treat or manage according to protocol and provider privileges.
Does it require longitudinal disease management? Coordinate with PCP while determining whether surgery must be delayed.
Does it exceed routine preoperative management? Refer to the appropriate specialist.
Does it affect anesthesia safety? Escalate to anesthesia.
Does it affect procedural timing or bleeding risk? Include the surgeon.
Is anything still unresolved? Assign Pending status until the required item is completed.

Key Takeaways

  • SPA providers should directly optimize routine reversible conditions within scope.
  • Not every abnormality requires PCP or specialist referral.
  • Use PCPs for longitudinal chronic-disease management when appropriate.
  • Use specialists for disease complexity, instability or questions beyond routine SPA management.
  • RNs can organize records, perform approved standing-order tasks and identify red flags.
  • Medical interpretation and final perioperative decisions belong to the appropriately credentialed provider.
  • Every pending item should have an owner and deadline.
  • Communicate anesthesia-specific risks directly with anesthesia.
  • Include surgeons when medical issues affect timing, bleeding risk or procedural venue.
  • Use Optimized, Pending or Not optimized rather than “cleared.”
