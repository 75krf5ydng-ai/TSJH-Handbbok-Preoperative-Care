Purpose

Use this page to decide whether existing testing remains clinically usable, whether repeat testing is indicated, and when abnormal findings require additional evaluation before elective noncardiac surgery.

Testing should be driven by the patient's medical condition, planned procedure, interval change in symptoms, medication changes and likelihood that the result will change perioperative management.

This handbook is a clinical decision-support resource and does not replace institutional policy, surgeon/anesthesia requirements, specialty recommendations or individualized clinical judgment.

Core Rule: When Can Prior Testing Be Used?

Prior testing is generally most useful when the patient's clinical condition is stable, no new symptoms have developed, no significant intervention has occurred since the study, and the test remains within an acceptable surveillance interval.

Repeat testing should be considered when:

  • New or worsening cardiopulmonary symptoms are present.
  • There has been a significant change in functional status.
  • A major medication or treatment change may affect the result.
  • A prior study was abnormal and the abnormality requires surveillance.
  • The prior study is outside the accepted institutional or specialty interval.
  • The planned surgical risk or anesthetic plan has materially changed.
  • A new diagnosis has been made since the prior study.

Cardiac Surveillance

Heart Failure / Cardiomyopathy

Repeat assessment is driven by new or worsening dyspnea, orthopnea, edema, exercise intolerance, hospitalization or other evidence of clinical decompensation.

Elective surgery should not proceed through unresolved acute decompensated heart failure.
Aortic Stenosis

Moderate-to-severe or symptomatic aortic stenosis warrants cardiovascular review and assessment of severity before elective surgery when current information is inadequate or symptoms have changed.

Severe disease includes findings such as valve area below approximately 1.0 cm², mean gradient above approximately 40 mmHg or peak velocity above approximately 4.0 m/s.

Mitral Stenosis

Moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis generally warrants cardiovascular evaluation with sufficiently current echocardiographic information.

Findings suggesting severe disease include mitral valve area at or below approximately 1.5 cm², significant mean gradient elevation or pulmonary hypertension.

Pulmonary Hypertension

Review right-sided function, pulmonary pressures, tricuspid regurgitation, WHO group and pulmonary vasodilator therapy.

Higher-level-care consideration is appropriate for severe pulmonary hypertension, marked RV dysfunction, severe TR, advanced pulmonary vasodilator therapy or very high RVSP.
Left Bundle Branch Block

New or clinically unexplained LBBB should be evaluated in the context of symptoms and cardiac history. When ischemia evaluation is required, pharmacologic nuclear stress testing may be preferred over exercise ECG interpretation alone.

TSJH / TSJR BNP Screening Protocol

Consider BNP or NT-proBNP when the patient meets institutional cardiac-risk screening criteria, including selected patients with elevated RCRI, older age or significant cardiovascular disease.
Marker Threshold Postoperative Action
BNP ≥ 92 pg/mL Postoperative ECG plus daily troponin surveillance for approximately 48–72 hours per protocol.
NT-proBNP ≥ 300 ng/L Postoperative ECG plus daily troponin surveillance for approximately 48–72 hours per protocol.

Common Reasons to Obtain Specialty Review

Finding Recommended Action
New chest pain or suspected acute coronary syndrome Defer elective procedure and obtain urgent cardiovascular evaluation.
Acute decompensated heart failure Optimize and obtain cardiovascular evaluation before elective surgery.
Symptomatic severe valvular disease Cardiovascular evaluation before elective surgery.
New significant arrhythmia Evaluate underlying cause and determine stability before elective surgery.
Severe pulmonary hypertension / RV dysfunction Consider specialty evaluation and appropriate higher-acuity surgical setting.
Unexplained hypoxemia or new oxygen requirement Pulmonary or medical evaluation and optimization before elective surgery.
Active infection or unstable systemic illness Treat and reassess appropriateness of elective surgery.
Severe uncontrolled anemia Define cause and optimize before elective surgery when clinically appropriate.

When a Normal Stress Test or Heart Catheterization Is Recent

There is generally no mandatory waiting period solely because a diagnostic stress test or diagnostic cardiac catheterization was performed, provided the patient is clinically stable, the study does not reveal a condition requiring treatment, no procedural complication occurred and the cardiology/anesthesia plan does not require delay.
A waiting period may be required after PCI, stent placement, myocardial infarction, vascular complication, initiation of dual-antiplatelet therapy or other therapeutic intervention. Follow current cardiology and perioperative guidance for the specific intervention.

Do Not Order Routine Testing Without an Indication

Routine testing may lead to incidental abnormalities, delays, cost and additional workup without improving perioperative outcomes.

  • Do not order routine stress testing in low-risk patients.
  • Do not order routine echocardiography in stable asymptomatic patients without a clinical indication.
  • Do not obtain routine chest radiography solely because of age.
  • Do not routinely screen asymptomatic patients for bacteriuria unless there is a procedure-specific indication.
  • Do not repeat stable testing solely because an arbitrary amount of time has passed unless policy or disease surveillance requires it.

Preoperative Testing Decision Checklist

  • Does the patient have new or worsening symptoms?
  • Has functional status changed?
  • Has there been a recent hospitalization?
  • Has treatment changed significantly?
  • Is an existing abnormality due for surveillance?
  • Will the result change perioperative management?
  • Is the test required by TSJH/TSJR policy or surgeon/anesthesia protocol?
  • Would a normal result allow surgery to proceed?
  • Would an abnormal result cause delay, treatment or higher-level-care planning?

Final Testing Status

Proceed Existing testing is adequate, condition is stable and no additional testing is clinically indicated.

Pending Additional testing, records or specialty interpretation is required before final optimization.

Not Optimized Active or unstable disease requires evaluation or treatment before elective surgery.
