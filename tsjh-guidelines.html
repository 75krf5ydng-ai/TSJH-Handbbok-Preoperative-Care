Pulmonary Hypertension / Right Heart

TSJH HLOC red flags:
  • WHO Group 1 pulmonary hypertension
  • RVSP >65 mmHg
  • Severe tricuspid regurgitation
  • Moderate-to-severe RV dysfunction
  • Advanced pulmonary vasodilator therapy

Any of these findings should trigger deliberate surgeon/anesthesia review and consideration of an alternate facility / higher level of care.

PCI / stent caution: do not interrupt required dual antiplatelet therapy after recent PCI without multidisciplinary review.
  • Continue aspirin when required for dual/triple therapy unless procedure-specific bleeding risk dictates otherwise.
  • Joints: aspirin often held approximately 5 days when interruption is appropriate.
  • Spine / SCS / IT procedures: aspirin often held approximately 7 days when interruption is appropriate.
  • Restart antiplatelet therapy as soon as surgical hemostasis permits.
  • Watchman / ablation: avoid interrupting required anticoagulation during the early post-procedure period without specialist guidance.
  • Use ManageAnticoag / MDCalc or specialist input when bridging decisions are needed.

GLP-1 / SGLT2 / Diabetes Medication Considerations

SGLT2

Confirm appropriate preoperative hold plan and assess for euglycemic ketoacidosis risk when clinically relevant.

GLP-1

Assess current GI symptoms, aspiration risk, dosing pattern, and current anesthesia guidance rather than applying a single rule without context.

Insulin Pump

Ensure a documented perioperative pump / glucose-management plan.

Hyperglycemia

Marked or symptomatic hyperglycemia requires evaluation and optimization before elective surgery.

Laboratory / Medical Optimization

Anemia

Review chronicity, expected surgical blood loss, MCV/iron studies when appropriate, renal disease, bleeding history, and cause. Optimize when clinically appropriate.

Polycythemia

Evaluate persistent elevated hemoglobin for dehydration, hypoxemia, OSA/COPD, testosterone/androgen exposure, renal/EPO-mediated causes, or myeloproliferative disease.

Electrolytes

Abnormal sodium or potassium should be interpreted by severity, symptoms, chronicity, etiology, and procedure risk. Repeat testing when clinically indicated.

Renal

Active/progressive AKI, major electrolyte disturbance, or unresolved dialysis timing requires optimization / review before final status.

Higher-Level-of-Care / Not Optimized Summary

Examples that should drive âNot optimized / higher level of careâ in the provider workflow:
  • LVEF <35%
  • Severe aortic stenosis by TSJH thresholds
  • WHO Group 1 pulmonary hypertension
  • RVSP >65 mmHg
  • Severe tricuspid regurgitation
  • Moderate-to-severe RV dysfunction
  • Advanced pulmonary vasodilator therapy
  • Other active/unstable medical conditions that cannot be appropriately managed in the planned setting
Pending: use when the patient may ultimately be appropriate for surgery but required testing, records, specialist/anesthesia review, medication planning, repeat labs, or other optimization items remain unresolved.
Optimized: use when no unresolved medical optimization, required testing, or TSJH higher-level-of-care criteria remain.
